# Medical quality audit

Generated: 2026-09-10

## Scope and evidence standard

This audit covers all 520 symptom chapters included by `medical_symptoms.tex`. Each chapter was screened for the required section structure, presence of references, terminology drift, and high-risk claim patterns. Medical claims were checked against current authoritative guidance where applicable, prioritizing WHO, NICE, CDC, NIH/MedlinePlus, and relevant specialty-society guidance, followed by systematic reviews and academic references. The WHO describes its guideline process as evidence-based and quality-assured, while MedlinePlus provides regularly reviewed patient information. Evidence availability and clinical certainty are not the same as structural completeness.

The targeted corrections addressed pediatric croup steroid overgeneralization; GERD endoscopy and reflux-monitoring claims; bowel-obstruction CT contrast wording; emergency red-eye treatment wording; acute sinus-pain imaging; hemorrhoid and sacroiliac-joint diagnostic terminology; varicocele, mania, sleep-apnea, snoring, polydipsia, steatorrhea, food-intolerance, rectal-bleeding, peptic-ulcer, and gastritis diagnostic/treatment claims; and stale front-matter titles/counts.

## Severity handling

- Critical: emergency or contraindicated advice requiring immediate correction.
- High: materially inaccurate diagnosis, treatment, dosing, or red-flag guidance.
- Moderate: important omission, weak evidence, or incomplete differential/evaluation.
- Low: wording, citation metadata, or minor consistency issue.

The automated screening is not a substitute for specialist peer review. Chapters marked “Evidence expansion needed” should receive additional disease-specific sources before publication, especially chapters covering emergency care, pregnancy, children, medication dosing, mental health crises, or invasive treatment.

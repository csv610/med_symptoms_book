\chapter{Bent Penis (Peyronie Disease)}

\section*{Definition}
Acquired curvature of the erect penis caused by fibrous plaque formation in the tunica albuginea of the corpora cavernosa, often associated with pain and erectile dysfunction.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Peyronie disease results from repetitive microvascular trauma or buckling injury to the erect penis, triggering an abnormal wound-healing response. Fibrin deposition leads to chronic inflammation, dysregulated collagen production, and plaque formation in the tunica albuginea. The inelastic plaque prevents uniform expansion of the tunica during erection, causing curvature toward the side of the plaque.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Peyronie disease (of unknown cause, most common)
 \item Penile trauma or fracture during intercourse
 \item Dupuytren contracture (associated condition)
 \item Genetic predisposition (family history)
 \item Connective tissue disorders
 \item Diabetes mellitus
 \item Autoimmune conditions
 \item Radiation therapy to the pelvis
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
 \item Sudden onset of penile curvature with severe pain, inability to have intercourse, or associated erectile dysfunction
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Erectile dysfunction
 \item Penile pain
 \item Penile shortening
 \item Dupuytren contracture
 \item Penile plaque
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} Your doctor will check for serious underlying conditions when evaluating persistent bent penis.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Avoid sexual activity or vigorous manipulation during the acute inflammatory phase (first 3–6 months) to prevent further plaque formation.
 \item Take over-the-counter NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) if pain is present during erections in the early inflammatory stage.
 \item Apply gentle traction or stretching exercises only if recommended by a specialist — do not attempt manual correction of curvature.
 \item Monitor curvature by taking monthly photographs at home to objectively track progression or stability over time.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Your doctor will take a detailed history of onset, progression, pain during erections, and impact on sexual function, and perform a focused genital examination.
 \item Palpation of the penile shaft identifies the location, size, and tenderness of the fibrous plaque within the tunica albuginea.
 \item Penile duplex Doppler ultrasound before and after pharmacologically induced erection measures plaque size and calcification, curvature angle, and vascular parameters (peak systolic velocity).
 \item Referral to a urologist specialising in sexual medicine is essential for confirmation of diagnosis and treatment planning.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Collagenase clostridium histolyticum (Xiapex) injections are FDA-approved for Peyronie disease and reduce curvature by enzymatic degradation of the plaque collagen.
 \item Vacuum erectile devices or simple traction therapy for 30–90 minutes daily may reduce curvature over several months in the chronic phase.
 \item Oral therapy with vitamin E, potassium para-aminobenzoate, or tamoxifen is commonly used but limited evidence supports efficacy.
 \item Surgical correction (plication, plaque incision and grafting, or penile prosthesis) is reserved for stable disease (at least 12 months) with curvature greater than 60 degrees or significant erectile dysfunction.
 \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
 \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

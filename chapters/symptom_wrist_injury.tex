\chapter{Wrist Injury}

\section*{Definition}
Wrist injuries involve damage to the bones, ligaments, tendons, or nerves of the wrist joint. The wrist is a complex joint composed of the radius, ulna, and eight carpal bones. Common injuries include fractures, sprains, and tendonitis.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
The wrist absorbs and transmits forces from the hand to the forearm. Fall onto an outstretched hand (FOOSH) is the classic mechanism of injury, causing fractures (distal radius, scaphoid) or ligament sprains. The scaphoid bone has a tenuous blood supply, and fractures can lead to avascular tissue death. Repetitive motions cause tendonitis (De Quervain tenosynovitis) or carpal tunnel syndrome from nerve compression.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Fall onto an outstretched hand (FOOSH): most common mechanism
\item Sports injuries: gymnastics, skating, football, basketball
\item Direct blow to the wrist
\item Repetitive motion: typing, assembly work, weightlifting
\item Osteoporosis (increases fracture risk in older adults)
\item Motor vehicle accidents
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Pain, swelling, and tenderness around the wrist
\item Decreased range of motion
\item Bruising or deformity
\item Numbness or tingling in the hand (possible nerve involvement)
\item Weakness in grip
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Wrist fracture (distal radius, scaphoid)
\item Carpal tunnel syndrome
\item De Quervain tenosynovitis
\item Wrist sprain
\item Arthritis of the wrist
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Apply ice and elevate the wrist
\item Immobilize with a splint or brace
\item Take OTC pain relievers
\item Rest and avoid activities that worsen pain
\item Be cautious of scaphoid fractures - any wrist pain in the anatomical snuffbox warrants evaluation
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Physical examination: inspection, palpation, range of motion, neurovascular assessment
\item X-ray: standard wrist views and scaphoid view
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Fracture: closed reduction and casting vs. open reduction with internal fixation
\item Ligament injury: immobilization vs. surgical repair
\item Tendonitis: rest, ice, NSAIDs, physical therapy, corticosteroid injection
\end{itemize}

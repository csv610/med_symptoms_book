\chapter{Nasal Congestion (Blocked Nose)}

\section*{Definition}
Obstruction of nasal airflow due to inflammation, swelling of nasal turbinates, mucus accumulation, or structural abnormalities, causing difficulty breathing through the nose.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Nasal congestion results from widening of blood vessels and engorgement of venous sinusoids in the nasal turbinates triggered by inflammation (allergic, infectious), autonomic dysregulation, or medication effects. Mucosal edema narrows nasal passages while increased secretions further obstruct airflow. Chronic causes include structural abnormalities (deviated septum, nasal polyps) or chronic rhinosinusitis.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Common cold (viral upper respiratory infection)
  \item Allergic rhinitis (hay fever)
  \item Sinusitis (acute or chronic)
  \item Deviated nasal septum
  \item Nasal polyps
  \item Vasomotor rhinitis
  \item Pregnancy rhinitis
  \item Rhinitis medicamentosa (decongestant spray overuse)
  \item Environmental irritants (smoke, pollution, dry air)
  \item Enlarged adenoids or tonsils (in children)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Nasal congestion with high fever, facial swelling, visual changes, or unilateral congestion with bloody discharge
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Runny nose
  \item Sneezing
  \item Sinus pressure
  \item Headache
  \item Reduced sense of smell
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Use saline nasal spray or perform nasal irrigation (Neti pot or squeeze bottle with distilled sterile water) 1-3 times daily to flush mucus and allergens.
  \item Apply a warm, damp towel over the sinuses or inhale steam from a bowl of hot water (carefully) to moisturise nasal passages and thin secretions.
  \item Use over-the-counter decongestant sprays (oxymetazoline) for no more than 3 consecutive days to avoid rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa).
  \item Elevate the head with an extra pillow at night to improve nasal drainage and reduce congestion while sleeping.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Anterior rhinoscopy using an otoscope or nasal speculum to assess nasal mucosa colour, turbinate size, and presence of polyps or discharge.
  \item Allergy testing (skin prick test or specific IgE) is performed if allergic rhinitis is suspected based on seasonal pattern and triggers.
  \item Nasal endoscopy (by an ENT specialist) to visualise the posterior nasal cavity, osteomeatal complex, and identify polyps, deviated septum, or sinus obstruction.
  \item CT sinuses without contrast is indicated for chronic or recurrent sinusitis to assess for sinus opacification and anatomical variants.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Allergic rhinitis is treated with intranasal corticosteroids (fluticasone, mometasone) as first-line daily therapy, combined with oral or intranasal antihistamines as needed.
  \item Acute infectious congestion is managed with saline irrigation, decongestants (oral pseudoephedrine or short-term topical), and steam inhalation.
  \item Chronic sinusitis may require prolonged antibiotics (if bacterial), intranasal corticosteroids, and sinus balloon dilation or functional endoscopic sinus surgery if medical therapy fails.
  \item Structural obstruction (deviated septum, polyps) is corrected surgically (septoplasty, polypectomy) when medical therapy is insufficient to relieve symptoms.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

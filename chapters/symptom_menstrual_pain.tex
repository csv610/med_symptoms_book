\chapter{Menstrual Pain (Dysmenorrhea)}

\section*{Definition}
Dysmenorrhea is painful menstruation that affects 50-90 percent of menstruating women. Primary dysmenorrhea is menstrual pain without underlying pathology. Secondary dysmenorrhea is caused by an identifiable condition such as endometriosis or fibroids.

\section*{Pathophysiology}
Primary dysmenorrhea results from prostaglandin F2-alpha release from the endometrium during menstruation, causing intense uterine contractions, ischemia, and pain. Secondary dysmenorrhea involves underlying pathology: endometriosis (ectopic endometrial tissue), adenomyosis (endometrial tissue in myometrium), uterine fibroids, or pelvic inflammatory disease.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Primary dysmenorrhea: prostaglandin-mediated uterine cramping
\item Endometriosis (ectopic endometrial implants)
\item Adenomyosis (endometrial tissue within the uterine wall)
\item Uterine fibroids (leiomyomas)
\item Pelvic inflammatory disease
\item Ovarian cysts
\item Cervical stenosis
\item Intrauterine device (IUD) use
\item Psychological factors (stress, anxiety)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Cramping pain in the lower abdomen just before or during menstruation
\item Pain radiating to the lower back or thighs
\item Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea during menstruation
\item Headache or fatigue
\item Pain that improves with age or after childbirth (primary)
\item Pain that worsens over time (secondary)
\item Heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia)
\item Pain with intercourse (dyspareunia)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia)
\item Irregular menstruation
\item Premenstrual syndrome (PMS)
\item Endometriosis
\item Uterine fibroids
\item Pelvic pain
\item Back pain
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care/Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Apply a heating pad or warm bath for relief
\item Take OTC NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) starting 1-2 days before menstruation
\item Exercise regularly (releases endorphins)
\item Practice relaxation techniques and stress management
\item Reduce salt, caffeine, and sugar intake before menses
\item Try dietary supplements (magnesium, vitamin B1, omega-3 fatty acids)
\item Rest when needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clinical history: timing, character, and severity of pain
\item Pelvic examination
\item Pelvic ultrasound to rule out secondary causes
\item Laparoscopy for definitive diagnosis of endometriosis
\item MRI for adenomyosis in selected cases
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item NSAIDs as first-line therapy
\item Combined oral contraceptive pills (suppress ovulation)
\item Progestin-only contraceptives (pills, IUD, implant)
\item GnRH agonists for endometriosis
\item Surgery: endometriosis excision, myomectomy, hysterectomy
\item Complementary therapies: acupuncture, transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation
\item Cognitive behavioral therapy for chronic pain
\end{itemize}

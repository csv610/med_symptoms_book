\chapter{Bloating}

\section*{Definition}
Abdominal distension, fullness, or tightness sensation caused by excess gas accumulation in the gastrointestinal tract, often accompanied by visible swelling of the abdomen.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Gas accumulation results from swallowed air (aerophagia), bacterial fermentation of undigested food in your colon, or impaired gas transit/evacuation. Visceral hypersensitivity amplifies the perception of normal gas volumes in you with functional bowel disorders.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)
 \item Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)
 \item Constipation
 \item Food intolerances (lactose, fructose, gluten)
 \item Swallowing air (aerophagia)
 \item Gastroparesis or delayed gastric emptying
 \item Ovarian pathology (cysts, masses)
 \item Celiac disease
 \item Gastrointestinal obstruction
 \item Ascites from liver disease or malignancy
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
 \item Persistent bloating lasting more than 2 weeks, bloating with abdominal pain, unexplained weight loss, or changes in bowel habits
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Abdominal pain
 \item Flatulence
 \item Constipation
 \item Diarrhea
 \item Nausea
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} Your doctor will check for serious underlying conditions when evaluating persistent bloating.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Eat smaller, more frequent meals rather than large portions to reduce gastric distension and gas production.
 \item Keep a food-symptom diary to identify trigger foods (common culprits include beans, lentils, cabbage, onions, carbonated drinks, and artificial sweeteners).
 \item Try an over-the-counter simethicone product for gas-related bloating or a trial of a low-FODMAP diet under guidance from a dietitian.
 \item Eat slowly, chew food thoroughly, and avoid drinking through straws or chewing gum to reduce aerophagia (swallowed air).
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Your doctor will take a dietary history and perform an abdominal examination assessing for distension, tympany, fluid wave (ascites), and palpable masses.
 \item Breath testing for lactose intolerance, fructose malabsorption, or small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) may be arranged based on symptom pattern.
 \item Abdominal ultrasound or CT scan is indicated if ascites, ovarian masses, or hepatosplenomegaly is suspected on examination.
 \item Referral to a gastroenterologist is arranged for upper endoscopy or colonoscopy if alarm symptoms (weight loss, anaemia, bleeding) are present.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Dietary modification including a low-FODMAP diet, elimination of identified trigger foods, or a gluten-free trial is first-line management for functional bloating.
 \item Rifaximin, a non-absorbable antibiotic, is used for SIBO confirmed by breath testing and typically given as a 14-day course.
 \item Prokinetic agents (metoclopramide, domperidone) or low-dose tricyclic antidepressants may be prescribed for gastroparesis or visceral hypersensitivity.
 \item Treatment of the underlying cause includes laxatives for constipation, pancreatic enzyme replacement for insufficiency, and diuretics for ascites.
 \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
 \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

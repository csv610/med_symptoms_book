\chapter{Burning Feet}

\section*{Definition}
A sensation of heat, burning, or warmth in the feet, often accompanied by tingling, numbness, or sharp pain, indicating underlying peripheral nerve dysfunction.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Burning feet most commonly result from peripheral neuropathy, particularly length-dependent axonal neuropathy where distal nerve endings are affected first. Metabolic (diabetes), toxic (alcohol), nutritional (B vitamin deficiencies), and inflammatory (autoimmune) causes damage to small nerve fibers (A-delta and C fibers) that transmit pain and temperature sensation, leading to aberrant nociceptive signaling.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (most common)
 \item Alcoholic neuropathy
 \item Vitamin B deficiency (B1, B6, B12)
 \item Peripheral arterial disease (critical limb ischemia)
 \item Tarsal tunnel syndrome
 \item Hypothyroidism
 \item Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy
 \item Chronic kidney disease (uremic neuropathy)
 \item Erythromelalgia (rare vascular disorder)
 \item Heavy metal toxicity (lead, mercury, arsenic)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
 \item Burning feet with uncontrolled diabetes, progressive weakness, skin breakdown, or weight loss
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Foot numbness
 \item Tingling
 \item Leg pain
 \item Skin changes
 \item Foot ulcers
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} Your doctor will check for serious underlying conditions when evaluating persistent burning feet.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Soak feet in cool (not ice-cold) water for 15–20 minutes when burning is severe — avoid hot water which may worsen symptoms.
 \item Wear well-cushioned, wide-toed shoes with moisture-wicking socks to reduce pressure and irritation on the feet.
 \item Examine feet daily for blisters, cuts, redness, or skin breakdown, especially if you have diabetes or reduced sensation.
 \item Avoid prolonged standing or walking on hard surfaces, and elevate feet when resting to improve circulation.
 \item Limit alcohol intake if alcoholic neuropathy is a suspected contributor, and consider a B-complex vitamin supplement.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Your doctor will take a history of alcohol use, diabetes, medication exposure (including chemotherapy), and perform a full neurological examination of the lower extremities.
 \item Laboratory evaluation includes fasting glucose or HbA1c, vitamin B12 level, thyroid function tests, renal function, and serum protein electrophoresis to screen for underlying causes.
 \item Nerve conduction studies and electromyography (EMG) are performed to confirm peripheral neuropathy and differentiate axonal from demyelinating patterns.
 \item Referral to a neurologist is arranged for unexplained or progressive neuropathy, and to a podiatrist for ongoing foot care if sensation is impaired.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Tight glycaemic control is the cornerstone of preventing and managing diabetic peripheral neuropathy, slowing progression and reducing pain.
 \item Neuropathic pain agents including gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine, and amitriptyline are first-line pharmacotherapy for painful neuropathy.
 \item Vitamin B12 replacement is given for documented deficiency, and alcohol cessation is essential for alcoholic neuropathy to prevent progression.
 \item Topical agents (capsaicin cream, lidocaine patches) and physical modalities (transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation, acupuncture) may provide adjunctive relief.
 \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
 \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

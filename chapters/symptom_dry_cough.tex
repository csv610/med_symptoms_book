\chapter{Dry Cough}

\section*{Definition}
A dry cough produces little or no mucus. It can result from airway irritation, viral infection, asthma, reflux, allergies, medication effects, or lung inflammation.

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
Seek urgent evaluation for Breathing difficulty, blue lips, coughing blood, severe chest pain, confusion, persistent fever, or a rapidly worsening cough. Arrange routine or prompt assessment for persistent, recurrent, unexplained, or function-limiting symptoms.

\section*{How It Develops}
A dry cough occurs when cough-sensitive nerves are irritated without much mucus. Viral infection, asthma, postnasal inflammation, reflux, medicines, and environmental irritants can all increase cough sensitivity.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Viral respiratory infection, asthma, postnasal inflammation, reflux, ACE inhibitors, environmental irritants, interstitial lung disease, and pertussis.
  \item Medication effects, environmental exposures, and chronic disease may contribute.
  \item Less common but important causes include malignancy, vascular disease, or organ failure when symptoms persist or progress.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Throat irritation, hoarseness, nasal symptoms, wheezing, breathlessness, or heartburn
  \item Fever, chest pain, coughing blood, night sweats, or weight loss
\end{itemize}

\section*{Medical Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item History addresses onset, duration, severity, triggers, exposures, medications, medical conditions, pregnancy status when relevant, and warning symptoms.
  \item Examination includes vital signs, oxygen saturation, the nose and throat, and the lungs.
  \item Chest imaging, spirometry, allergy or reflux evaluation, and other tests are selected according to duration and suspected cause; they are not all routine.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
Treatment depends on the cause: remove irritants, treat asthma or nasal disease when present, review medicines such as ACE inhibitors, and address reflux only when supported by symptoms. Persistent cough needs reassessment rather than repeated empiric treatment.

\section*{Self-Care and Home Management}
Avoid known triggers, maintain reasonable hydration, record timing and associated symptoms, and use only treatments appropriate for the suspected cause. Do not use leftover antibiotics or delay urgent care because symptoms temporarily improve.

\chapter{Anxiety}

\section*{Definition}
Anxiety represents excessive worry, nervousness, or unease about imminent events or uncertainties, often disproportionate to actual threat level, potentially interfering with daily functioning distinguishing transient fear states from generalized anxiety disorder panic disorder specific phobias clinical entities.

\section*{Pathophysiology}
Anxiety involves complex neurocircuitry primarily centered on amygdala hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis limbic system interactions Amygdala processes threat signals projecting to hypothalamus activating sympathetic nervous system releasing catecholamines cortisol HPA axis dysregulation observed chronic anxiety disorders Prefrontal cortex normally exerts top-down inhibition over amygdala diminished in GABAergic dysfunction serotonin norepinephrine dopamine imbalances contribute pathological anxiety states Central sensitization lowers threshold perceiving threats triggering exaggerated physiological responses including tachycardia hypertension sweating tremor respiratory changes gastrointestinal disturbances.

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
 \item Worry persisting beyond several weeks uncontrollable by reassurance
 \item Sleep disturbance difficulty falling/staying asleep frequent awakening
 \item Physical symptoms tachypnea palpitations muscle tension GI upset tremor tingling or numbness
 \item Functional impairment work school social relationships daily activities
 \item Safety checking behaviors reassurance seeking avoidance patterns escalating
 \item Suicidal ideation hopelessness accompanying severe depression anxiety comorbidity
 \item Panic attack frequency increasing or severity intensifying requiring emergency evaluation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Establish a consistent daily routine with regular sleep, meals, and physical activity to support mental health stability.
 \item Practice stress management techniques including mindfulness, deep breathing exercises, and progressive muscle relaxation.
 \item Maintain social connections and avoid isolation by reaching out to trusted friends, family, or support groups.
 \item Avoid alcohol, caffeine, and recreational drugs which can destabilize mood and exacerbate psychiatric symptoms.
 \item If experiencing suicidal thoughts or crisis, contact emergency services or a crisis helpline immediately.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Comprehensive psychiatric assessment includes history of present illness, past psychiatric history, substance use, medical history, and collateral information from family.
 \item Standardized screening tools (GAD-7, PHQ-9, PCL-5) help quantify symptom severity and track treatment response.
 \item Medical evaluation (thyroid function, basic metabolic panel, CBC, toxicology screen) rules out organic causes of psychiatric symptoms.
 \item Risk assessment for self-harm, suicide, and danger to others is an essential component of every psychiatric evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Psychotherapy (CBT, DBT, interpersonal therapy) is a first-line treatment for most psychiatric disorders.
 \item Pharmacotherapy with antidepressants, anxiolytics, mood stabilizers, or antipsychotics is tailored to the specific diagnosis and individual patient factors.
 \item Combined treatment (medication plus psychotherapy) generally yields superior outcomes compared to either modality alone.
 \item Referral to psychiatry is indicated for severe symptoms, treatment resistance, suicidal ideation, or diagnostic uncertainty.
\end{itemize}

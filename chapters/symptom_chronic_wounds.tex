\chapter{Chronic Wounds}

\section*{Definition}
Wounds that fail to proceed through orderly healing stages within 4-6 weeks, including pressure ulcers, venous stasis ulcers, diabetic foot ulcers, and arterial insufficiency ulcers.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Healing failure results from prolonged inflammation, impaired angiogenesis, bacterial biofilm formation, and matrix metalloproteinase imbalance. Underlying causes include ischemia (arterial disease), venous hypertension (venous insufficiency), neuropathy and pressure (diabetes), or prolonged pressure (immobility). Biofilm bacteria evade immune clearance and resist antibiotics.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Venous insufficiency (venous stasis ulcers)
  \item Peripheral arterial disease (ischemic ulcers)
  \item Diabetes mellitus (diabetic foot ulcers)
  \item Prolonged pressure (pressure ulcers/bedsores)
  \item Chronic infection or osteomyelitis
  \item Vasculitis or autoimmune disease
  \item Malignancy (basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma)
  \item Radiation therapy damage
  \item Lymphedema
  \item Pyoderma gangrenosum
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Any non-healing wound persisting more than 4 weeks, wound with increasing pain, redness, swelling, or purulent drainage
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Leg swelling
  \item Skin discoloration
  \item Pain
  \item Fever
  \item Reduced mobility
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Clean the wound gently with saline or clean water daily and apply a sterile, non-adherent dressing to maintain a moist wound-healing environment.
  \item Keep the wound and surrounding skin dry between dressing changes and inspect daily for signs of infection (increasing redness, warmth, swelling, purulent drainage, foul odour).
  \item Elevate the affected limb when resting to reduce oedema and improve venous return if the wound is on a lower extremity.
  \item Optimise nutrition with adequate protein intake (1.2–1.5 g/kg daily) and supplementation of vitamins A, C, and zinc which are essential for wound healing.
  \item Relieve pressure on the wound site by repositioning every 2 hours if immobile, using pressure-relieving mattresses or cushions as needed.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Your doctor will assess wound size, depth, exudate, bed tissue type (granulation, slough, eschar), and the condition of surrounding skin.
  \item Ankle-brachial pressure index (ABPI) is measured to differentiate venous from arterial ulcers and determine whether compression therapy is safe.
  \item Wound swab for bacterial culture is performed if infection is suspected; deep tissue biopsy may be needed for suspected osteomyelitis or malignancy.
  \item Referral to a wound care specialist or multidisciplinary wound clinic is arranged for non-healing wounds persisting beyond 4 weeks of standard care.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Compression therapy (multilayer bandaging or compression stockings) is the cornerstone of venous ulcer management, improving venous return and healing rates.
  \item Debridement (sharp, enzymatic, autolytic, or larval) is performed to remove necrotic tissue and biofilm that impede wound healing.
  \item Advanced wound dressings (hydrocolloids, alginates, foams, silver-impregnated) are selected based on wound exudate level, depth, and presence of infection.
  \item Revascularisation (angioplasty or bypass) is indicated for arterial ulcers with significant peripheral arterial disease confirmed by ABPI or angiography.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

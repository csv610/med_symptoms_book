\chapter{Neck Pain}

\section*{Definition}
Pain or discomfort localized to the cervical spine region, from the base of the skull to the upper shoulders, arising from musculoskeletal, neurological, or referred sources.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Neck pain most commonly results from mechanical dysfunction: muscle strain, ligamentous sprain, or facet joint irritation. Degenerative changes (cervical spondylosis) involve disc desiccation, osteophyte formation, and facet hypertrophy, which may lead to foraminal stenosis and nerve root compression (cervical radiculopathy). Myelopathy from spinal cord compression is a serious but less common cause.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Mechanical neck pain (muscle strain, poor posture)
  \item Cervical spondylosis (degenerative disc disease)
  \item Cervical radiculopathy (herniated disc, foraminal stenosis)
  \item Whiplash injury (motor vehicle accident)
  \item Tension-type headache (referred to neck)
  \item Meningitis (with fever, photophobia, Kernig sign)
  \item Cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression)
  \item Fibromyalgia or myofascial pain syndrome
  \item Rheumatoid arthritis (atlantoaxial instability)
  \item Vertebral artery dissection (rare, with neurological symptoms)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Neck pain with fever and stiff neck (possible meningitis), neck pain after trauma, or neck pain with arm weakness/numbness
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Headache
  \item Shoulder pain
  \item Arm pain or numbness
  \item Stiffness
  \item Limited range of motion
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Apply heat (heating pad, warm shower) for 15-20 minutes to relax tense neck muscles; ice may be used for acute injury within the first 48 hours.
  \item Maintain good posture during daily activities — keep ears aligned over shoulders, avoid jutting the chin forward, and take breaks from looking down at screens.
  \item Perform gentle neck range-of-motion exercises (chin tucks, side bends, slow rotations) several times daily to prevent stiffness.
  \item Use a supportive pillow (contoured cervical pillow) that maintains neutral spine alignment during sleep; avoid sleeping on your stomach.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Neurological examination including cervical spine range of motion, Spurling test (for cervical radiculopathy), upper limb strength, reflexes, sensation, and Hoffman sign (for myelopathy).
  \item Plain cervical spine X-rays (AP, lateral, open-mouth odontoid) with flexion-extension views if instability is suspected after trauma.
  \item MRI of the cervical spine is indicated for radiculopathy not responding to conservative therapy, suspected myelopathy, or red flags (malignancy, infection).
  \item CT cervical spine is preferred in acute trauma for assessing fractures and is faster than MRI in emergency settings.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Mechanical neck pain is treated with NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, heat therapy, and gentle mobilisation; a soft cervical collar should not be worn for more than 48 hours.
  \item Physical therapy including cervical strengthening, postural retraining, and manual therapy is first-line for recurrent or chronic mechanical neck pain.
  \item Cervical radiculopathy may benefit from epidural steroid injections or surgical decompression (discectomy, foraminotomy) if conservative therapy fails.
  \item Cervical myelopathy requires urgent neurosurgical referral for decompression to prevent irreversible spinal cord injury.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

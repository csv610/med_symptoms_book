\chapter{Nail Psoriasis}

\section{Definition}
Nail psoriasis is a manifestation of psoriasis affecting the fingernails or toenails. It occurs in up to 50 percent of people with cutaneous psoriasis and can occur in isolation. Nail psoriasis significantly impacts quality of life and is often difficult to treat.

\section{Pathophysiology}
Psoriasis involves immune-mediated inflammation driven by T-cells. In the nail, inflammation affects the nail matrix and nail bed. Matrix involvement causes pitting, leukonychia, and crumbling. Nail bed involvement causes onycholysis (separation from nail bed), oil-drop discoloration, splinter hemorrhages, and subungual hyperkeratosis.

\section{Etiology}
\begin{itemize}
\item Psoriasis vulgaris (chronic plaque psoriasis)
\item Psoriatic arthritis (nail changes are more common in psoriatic arthritis)
\item Genetic predisposition
\item Trauma (Koebner phenomenon: psoriasis develops at sites of injury)
\item Infection (streptococcal infection can trigger flares)
\item Stress
\item Medications (lithium, beta-blockers, antimalarials)
\end{itemize}

\section{Indications for Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
\item Small pits or depressions in the nail surface
\item Thickening and crumbling of the nail
\item Yellowish-brown oil-drop discoloration under the nail
\item Separation of the nail from the nail bed (onycholysis)
\item White patches or streaks on the nail
\item Subungual hyperkeratosis (debris under the nail)
\item Redness and swelling of the nail fold
\item Pain or discomfort in the nail area
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Psoriasis (skin plaques)
\item Psoriatic arthritis
\item Fungal nail infection (may coexist)
\item Paronychia (inflammation of nail fold)
\item Eczema of the hands
\end{itemize}

\section{Self-Care/Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Keep nails short and trimmed
\item Avoid trauma to the nails
\item Wear gloves for household work and gardening
\item Moisturize hands and nails regularly
\item Use nail polish to cosmetically conceal changes
\item Avoid picking or manipulating the nails
\item Gently clean under the nails
\end{itemize}

\section{Diagnostic Approach}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clinical examination of the nails
\item Examination of skin for psoriatic plaques
\item Dermoscopy of the nail
\item Nail clipping or biopsy if diagnosis is uncertain
\item Fungal culture to rule out coexistent onychomycosis
\end{itemize}

\section{Treatment/Management Overview}
\begin{itemize}
\item Topical corticosteroids with occlusion
\item Calcipotriol (vitamin D analogue)
\item Topical calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus)
\item Intralesional corticosteroid injection
\item Systemic therapy: methotrexate, cyclosporine, apremilast, biologics (adalimumab, secukinumab, ixekizumab)
\item Excimer laser or PUVA phototherapy
\item Treatment of associated skin and joint psoriasis
\end{itemize}

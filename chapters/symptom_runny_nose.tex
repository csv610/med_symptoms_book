\chapter{Runny Nose (Rhinorrhea)}

\section*{Definition}
Excessive discharge of nasal mucus from the nostrils, varying in consistency from clear and watery to thick and purulent, reflecting underlying causes.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Increased nasal secretion from goblet cells and submucosal glands triggered by viral infection, allergic response (IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation), vasomotor instability, or irritation. Clear rhinorrhea suggests allergy or viral causes; purulent discharge suggests bacterial sinusitis.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Common cold (viral URI)
  \item Allergic rhinitis (hay fever)
  \item Sinusitis (acute or chronic)
  \item Vasomotor rhinitis
  \item Cold weather or temperature changes
  \item Spicy foods (gustatory rhinitis)
  \item Nasal polyps
  \item Cerebrospinal fluid leak (clear watery unilateral)
  \item Pregnancy rhinitis
  \item Medication-induced (rhinitis medicamentosa from decongestant overuse)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Persistent symptoms beyond 10 days, thick yellow/green discharge with facial pain, fever, bloody discharge, or unilateral clear drainage after head trauma
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Sneezing
  \item Nasal congestion
  \item Sore throat
  \item Cough
  \item Itchy eyes
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Use saline nasal spray or a neti pot with sterile or distilled water two to three times daily to rinse allergens, viruses, and thickened mucus from nasal passages.
  \item Drink warm fluids (herbal tea, broth) and use a humidifier or steam inhalation to thin secretions and soothe irritated nasal mucosa.
  \item For allergic rhinitis: oral non-sedating antihistamines (cetirizine 10 mg, loratadine 10 mg, fexofenadine 180 mg daily) with or without intranasal corticosteroids.
  \item Avoid triggers: dust, pollen, pet dander, smoke, and strong odours; use a HEPA filter in the bedroom.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Anterior rhinoscopy and nasal speculum examination: assess nasal mucosa colour, discharge character, and presence of polyps.
  \item Allergy testing (skin prick test or serum-specific IgE) if allergic rhinitis is suspected and symptoms persist despite empiric therapy.
  \item CT sinuses if sinusitis is suspected (facial pain, purulent discharge lasting over ten days, fever, or recurrent episodes).
  \item For unilateral, clear, watery rhinorrhea: collect fluid for beta-2 transferrin assay to rule out CSF leak, especially with history of head trauma or meningitis.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Allergic rhinitis: intranasal corticosteroid (fluticasone two sprays each nostril daily for a minimum of two weeks) is first-line; add oral antihistamine or montelukast 10 mg if needed.
  \item Acute bacterial sinusitis: amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg twice daily for five to seven days for moderate symptoms; amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily for mild cases.
  \item Vasomotor rhinitis: intranasal ipratropium 0.03\% two sprays three times daily as needed to reduce hypersecretion.
  \item Refractory cases: referral to otolaryngology for sinus surgery (FESS), turbinate reduction, or immunotherapy for severe allergic rhinitis.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Nerve Pain (Neuropathic Pain)}

\section*{Definition}
Pain is caused by damage or dysfunction of the somatosensory nervous system, characterized by burning, shooting, electric shock-like, or stabbing sensations.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Nerve injury leads to abnormal ectopic discharge from damaged axons, altered ion channel expression, central sensitization in the spinal cord dorsal horn, and reduced descending inhibitory control. Neuroplastic changes in the brain amplify pain perception, making neuropathic pain often chronic and difficult to treat.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Diabetic peripheral neuropathy
  \item Postherpetic neuralgia (shingles)
  \item Trigeminal neuralgia
  \item Sciatica or lumbar radiculopathy
  \item Carpal tunnel syndrome
  \item Multiple sclerosis
  \item Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy
  \item HIV-associated neuropathy
  \item Alcohol-related neuropathy
  \item Complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item New or progressive burning or shooting pain, pain with weakness, numbness, or changes in bladder/bowel function
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Numbness
  \item Tingling
  \item Burning sensation
  \item Weakness
  \item Allodynia (pain to light touch)
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Protect the affected area from stimuli that worsen pain — avoid tight clothing, temperature extremes, and pressure on sensitive skin.
  \item Apply topical treatments such as lidocaine 5\% plaster or capsaicin cream (0.075\% or 8\% patch) directly to the painful area for localised relief.
  \item Practise good sleep hygiene and stress management, as sleep deprivation and stress amplify central sensitisation and lower the pain threshold.
  \item Engage in gentle movement and graded exercise as tolerated — immobilisation worsens neuropathic pain, while gradual activity helps desensitise the nervous system.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Neurological examination including sensory mapping (pinprick, light touch, vibration, temperature), motor strength, deep tendon reflexes, and provocative tests (Tinel sign for entrapment neuropathies).
  \item Nerve conduction studies and electromyography (NCS/EMG) to confirm nerve dysfunction, localise the lesion, and differentiate axonal from demyelinating injury.
  \item Blood work: fasting glucose and HbA1c (diabetic neuropathy), vitamin B12, thyroid function, and serum protein electrophoresis if indicated.
  \item MRI of the spine or relevant nerve plexus is performed when structural compression (disc herniation, foraminal stenosis, tumour) is suspected.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item First-line pharmacological therapy includes gabapentinoids (gabapentin, pregabalin) and SNRIs (duloxetine, venlafaxine) for most forms of neuropathic pain.
  \item Tricyclic antidepressants (amitriptyline, nortriptyline) are effective for diabetic neuropathy and postherpetic neuralgia but may be limited by anticholinergic side effects.
  \item Topical agents (lidocaine 5\% plaster, capsaicin 8\% patch) provide localised relief without systemic effects for focal neuropathic pain.
  \item Surgical decompression (carpal tunnel release, microdiscectomy for radiculopathy) or neuromodulation (spinal cord stimulation) is reserved for refractory cases with a defined structural lesion.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

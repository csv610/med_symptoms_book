\chapter{Balanitis}

\section*{Definition}
Balanitis is inflammation of the glans penis, often involving the foreskin (balanoposthitis). It is characterized by erythema, swelling, pain, and discharge on the glans, with recurrent episodes common in uncircumcised men.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Inflammation of the glans results from disruption of the normal mucosal barrier, allowing microbial overgrowth or an irritant contact reaction. Poor hygiene, moisture trapped beneath the foreskin, and impaired immune defenses facilitate colonization by Candida species, bacteria, or other pathogens.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Infectious:} Candidal infection (most common, especially in diabetes or after antibiotics), bacterial (Group B Streptococcus, Gardnerella, anaerobes), trichomoniasis, herpes simplex, syphilis, human papillomavirus
 \item \textbf{Irritant:} Soaps, detergents, lubricants, spermicides, excessive washing, chlorinated water
 \item \textbf{Dermatologic:} Contact dermatitis, lichen planus, lichen sclerosus (balanitis xerotica obliterans), psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, fixed drug eruption
 \item \textbf{Systemic:} Type 2 diabetes (especially with poor glycemic control), reactive arthritis (circinate balanitis), Reiter syndrome
 \item \textbf{Anatomical:} Phimosis (tight foreskin trapping debris), poor hygiene
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
 \item Severe pain, swelling, discharge, or difficulty urinating
 \item Recurrent episodes despite improved hygiene
 \item Associated with fever, systemic symptoms, or suspected sexually transmitted infection
 \item Foreskin cannot be retracted, especially in children (may indicate phimosis)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Phimosis, paraphimosis, penile discharge, painful urination, penile rash, genital itching, meatal stenosis
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} Recurrent balanitis may be the first manifestation of type 2 diabetes, particularly in middle-aged men.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Clean the glans gently with warm water and avoid harsh soaps
 \item Retract the foreskin daily for cleaning and drying
 \item Apply an over-the-counter antifungal cream (clotrimazole) if Candida is suspected
 \item Avoid sexual activity until inflammation resolves
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Visual inspection: note erythema, swelling, discharge, fissures, phimosis, or lesions
 \item Swab for microscopy, culture, and KOH prep (Candida)
 \item Screen for sexually transmitted infections if indicated by history
 \item Fasting blood glucose or HbA1c to rule out diabetes
 \item Biopsy if persistent, atypical, or suspected lichen sclerosus or malignancy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Antifungal: topical clotrimazole or miconazole for candidal balanitis
 \item Antibacterial: topical metronidazole or clindamycin for bacterial causes
 \item Steroid: low-potency topical hydrocortisone for inflammatory or dermatologic causes
 \item For recurrent cases with phimosis: circumcision is curative in many cases
 \item Optimize glycemic control if diabetes is present
\end{itemize}

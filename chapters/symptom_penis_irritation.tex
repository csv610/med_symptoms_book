\chapter{Penis Irritation (Balanitis)}

\section*{Definition}
Inflammation or irritation of the glans penis and/or foreskin (balanoposthitis), characterized by redness, swelling, itching, discharge, and discomfort.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Irritation results from infectious (candidal, bacterial, viral, parasitic), inflammatory (lichen sclerosus, contact dermatitis, psoriasis), or traumatic (friction, chemical irritation) causes. Poor hygiene, diabetes, and immunosuppression predispose to infection. Phimosis (tight foreskin) traps secretions increasing irritation risk.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Candidal infection (yeast — especially in diabetes)
  \item Poor hygiene (smegma accumulation)
  \item Contact dermatitis (soaps, detergents, lubricants, latex condoms)
  \item Bacterial infection (streptococcal, staphylococcal, anaerobic)
  \item Sexually transmitted infections (HSV, gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis)
  \item Lichen sclerosus (chronic inflammatory skin condition)
  \item Psoriasis or reactive arthritis
  \item Trauma or friction (vigorous intercourse, masturbation)
  \item Phimosis or Tight foreskin
  \item Allergic reaction (medications, topical creams)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Penis irritation with discharge, fever, difficulty urinating, or inability to retract foreskin (paraphimosis)
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Penile discharge
  \item Dysuria
  \item Swelling
  \item Itching
  \item Painful urination
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Gently wash the glans and foreskin with warm water daily; avoid soap, scented products, and vigorous scrubbing that can worsen irritation.
  \item Keep the area dry after washing; if uncircumcised, retract the foreskin, dry thoroughly, and return to its normal position to prevent paraphimosis.
  \item Apply a barrier cream (zinc oxide or petroleum jelly) to soothe irritated skin; avoid topical antifungals or steroids without a confirmed diagnosis.
  \item Discontinue use of potential irritants: new soaps, laundry detergents, lubricants, latex condoms (switch to non-latex), and spermicides.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Visual inspection of the glans, foreskin, and urethral meatus; note discharge, erythema, erosions, phimosis, or lesions suggestive of STI (vesicles, ulcers, warts).
  \item Urethral swab or first-void urine for chlamydia and gonorrhoea NAAT; HSV PCR of any vesicles or ulcers; syphilis serology if indicated.
  \item Swab for bacterial culture and sensitivity if purulent discharge is present; KOH preparation and fungal culture if candidal balanitis is suspected.
  \item Screen for diabetes with fasting glucose or HbA1c, especially in recurrent or refractory cases; consider biopsy if lichen sclerosus or malignancy is suspected.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item For candidal balanitis: topical clotrimazole 1\% cream twice daily for 7--14 days; oral fluconazole 150 mg single dose for severe or recurrent cases.
  \item For bacterial balanitis: topical antibiotic (mupirocin 2\% three times daily for seven days for staphylococcal; metronidazole gel 0.75\% twice daily for anaerobic).
  \item For contact dermatitis: topical hydrocortisone 1\% twice daily for five to seven days; eliminate the offending agent.
  \item For STIs: treat per guidelines (ceftriaxone 500 mg IM for gonorrhoea, doxycycline 100 mg BID for seven days for chlamydia); arrange sexual partner treatment and safe sex counselling.
\end{itemize}

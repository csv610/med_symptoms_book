\chapter{Yellow Tongue}

\section*{Definition}
Yellowish discoloration of the dorsal tongue surface, usually resulting from accumulation of bacteria, debris, or staining on elongated filiform papillae.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Yellow tongue typically arises from overgrowth of chromogenic bacteria or fungi (often Candida) on the tongue dorsum, especially when filiform papillae are elongated (hairy tongue). Elongation occurs when normal desquamation is impaired by poor oral hygiene, dry mouth, soft diet, or medications. The yellow color may also result from staining by food, tobacco, or mouthwashes containing oxidizing agents.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Poor oral hygiene (bacterial overgrowth)
  \item Dry mouth (dry mouth)
  \item Dehydration
  \item Smoking or tobacco use
  \item Mouthwashes containing peroxide or witch hazel
  \item Oral thrush (Candida overgrowth, can appear yellow)
  \item Coffee, tea, or food staining
  \item Antibiotic use (altered oral flora)
  \item Fever or illness (reduced oral clearance)
  \item yellowing of the skin or eyes (rare — sclera also yellow, bilirubin elevated)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Yellow tongue persisting more than 2 weeks despite good oral hygiene, or with yellowing of the skin or eyes, pain, or difficulty eating
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Bad breath
  \item White tongue
  \item Dry mouth
  \item Taste changes
  \item Oral thrush
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Gently brush the tongue dorsum with a soft toothbrush or use a tongue scraper twice daily to reduce elongated papillae and accumulated debris.
  \item Increase fluid intake (six to eight glasses of water daily) and chew sugar-free gum to stimulate saliva production and promote natural tongue cleaning.
  \item Discontinue use of mouthwashes containing peroxide, witch hazel, or oxidising agents that can stain the tongue yellow.
  \item Reduce coffee and tea consumption and stop smoking or using chewing tobacco to prevent extrinsic staining.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Clinical examination: inspect the tongue dorsum for elongated filiform papillae (hairy tongue), yellow coating, and distribution; assess for other oral lesions.
  \item Evaluate medications and habits: recent antibiotics, bismuth subsalicylate, oral contraceptives, mouthwash use, smoking, and dietary staining.
  \item KOH scrapings if Candida overgrowth is suspected; check for associated factors (xerostomia, diabetes, immunosuppression).
  \item If jaundice is suspected (yellowing of skin or sclera, dark urine): serum bilirubin, liver enzymes (AST, ALT, ALP, GGT), and hepatitis serology.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Hairy tongue (most common cause): improved oral hygiene, tongue scraping, and discontinuation of precipitating agents; usually resolves spontaneously over days to weeks.
  \item If oral thrush is confirmed: antifungal therapy with nystatin suspension or fluconazole as per candidiasis protocol.
  \item If discolouration is due to bismuth subsalicylate: reassurance that this is benign and resolves upon discontinuation of the medication.
  \item If jaundice is present: treat underlying hepatocellular disease or obstructive jaundice (ERCP for gallstones or strictures); referral to gastroenterology.
\end{itemize}

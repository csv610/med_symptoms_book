\chapter{Abdominal Pain}

\section*{Definition}
Abdominal pain is discomfort anywhere between the lower chest and groin. Its location, severity, and associated symptoms can help identify the cause.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Location, character, timing provide diagnostic clues distinguishing visceral vs somatic pain.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Gastroenteritis
 \item Appendicitis
 \item Peptic ulcer disease
 \item IBS
 \item Gallbladder disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Seek emergency care for sudden severe pain, a rigid or markedly tender abdomen, fainting, confusion, persistent vomiting, vomiting blood, black or bloody stools, chest pain, or possible pregnancy with pain or bleeding.
 \item Arrange prompt medical assessment for fever, jaundice, dehydration, persistent pain, or pain that is worsening or localized to one area.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Nausea
 \item Vomiting
 \item Bloating
 \item Anorexia
 \item Constipation/Diarrhea
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item For mild, improving pain without warning signs, drink fluids and eat small, bland meals as tolerated.
 \item Avoid alcohol and foods that clearly worsen symptoms.
 \item Acetaminophen may help when used exactly as labelled; avoid NSAIDs if ulcer, kidney disease, bleeding risk, or pregnancy is possible unless a clinician advises otherwise.
 \item Do not use laxatives or anti-diarrheal medicines when obstruction or an inflammatory/infectious cause is possible without medical advice.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item A focused history assesses onset, location, quality, severity, duration, radiation, aggravating/relieving factors, and prior treatments.
 \item Physical examination assesses hydration, abdominal tenderness, guarding, rigidity, distension, bowel sounds, hernias, and sometimes a pelvic or rectal source.
 \item Pregnancy testing, blood and urine tests, and imaging such as ultrasound or CT are selected according to age, pregnancy status, examination, and suspected cause.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Treatment depends on the cause and may include fluids, antiemetics, acid suppression, antibiotics, or urgent surgery or other procedures.
 \item Suspected appendicitis, obstruction, perforation, ectopic pregnancy, ischemia, or internal bleeding requires urgent clinician-directed treatment.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Acanthosis Nigricans}

\section*{Definition}
Acanthosis nigricans (AN) is a skin disorder characterized by symmetric, hyperpigmented, velvety plaques in intertriginous areas such as the neck, axillae, and groin. It is most commonly a marker of insulin resistance, but may also signal malignancy, endocrinopathy, or medication effect.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Insulin resistance leads to compensatory hyperinsulinemia, which activates insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) receptors on keratinocytes and dermal fibroblasts. This stimulates epidermal proliferation and papillomatosis, producing the characteristic velvety texture and hyperpigmentation. In malignant AN, tumor-secreted growth factors (TGF-\ensuremath{\alpha}, EGF) drive similar epidermal changes through paracrine signaling.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Metabolic:} Type 2 diabetes mellitus, metabolic syndrome, obesity, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
 \item \textbf{Genetic:} Familial AN, insulin receptor mutations, Alstr\"om syndrome
 \item \textbf{Malignant:} Gastric adenocarcinoma (most common), other GI adenocarcinomas, lung, ovarian, lymphoma
 \item \textbf{Medication-induced:} Oral contraceptives, glucocorticoids, niacin, growth hormone therapy, insulin
 \item \textbf{Endocrine:} Cushing's syndrome, acromegaly, hypothyroidism, Addison's disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
 \item Sudden onset or rapid progression, especially in a non-overweight, older adult (suggests malignancy)
 \item Extensive mucocutaneous involvement (oral, eyelids, palms) --- the sign of Leser-Tr\'elat
 \item Concurrent unintended weight loss, lymphadenopathy, or gastrointestinal symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Obesity, skin tags (acrochordons), hyperinsulinemia, hyperglycemia, hirsutism, acrochordons
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} In benign AN, treatment of the underlying insulin resistance with weight loss and metformin often improves the skin findings, though complete resolution is uncommon.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Weight reduction through diet and exercise to improve insulin sensitivity
 \item Topical moisturizers containing ammonium lactate 12\% or urea cream may improve texture
 \item Avoid repeated friction or irritation of affected intertriginous areas
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid panel, and insulin levels to assess insulin resistance
 \item Consider PCOS evaluation in women: free testosterone, LH/FSH ratio, pelvic ultrasound
 \item If malignancy suspected (atypical AN): CT chest/abdomen/pelvis, upper endoscopy, colonoscopy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Lifestyle modification and metformin for insulin-resistant AN; treat underlying endocrinopathy
 \item Topical therapies: Tretinoin 0.05\% cream, calcipotriol, or dual-agent combination creams
 \item Procedural: Cryotherapy, laser ablation (fractional CO2 or Er:YAG) for cosmetic improvement
 \item Surgical resection of underlying malignancy in paraneoplastic AN
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Facial Numbness}

\section*{Definition}
Facial numbness is a loss or reduced sensation on part or all of your face. It can affect one side or both sides and may range from a mild tingling to complete loss of feeling. The numbness follows the pattern of the trigeminal nerve, which carries sensation from your face to your brain.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Sensation from your face travels through the trigeminal nerve (the fifth cranial nerve) to your brain. When this nerve is compressed, inflamed, damaged, or its signals are interrupted anywhere along the pathway, you lose normal sensation. The cause may be in the nerve itself, the brainstem, or the part of the brain that processes touch signals.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bell's palsy:} Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the face, often improving within weeks to months
  \item \textbf{Multiple sclerosis:} Damage to the protective covering of nerves in the brain, which can cause facial numbness along with other symptoms
  \item \textbf{Trigeminal neuralgia:} Brief, intense, electric-shock-like facial pain that may be accompanied by numbness between attacks
  \item \textbf{Stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA):} Sudden numbness on one side of the face, often with arm weakness or speech difficulty
  \item \textbf{Shingles (herpes zoster):} Painful rash with numbness, usually on one side of the face
  \item \textbf{Dental problems:} Infection, abscess, or dental procedure can temporarily numb the face
  \item \textbf{Acoustic neuroma (vestibular schwannoma):} A benign tumor on the nerve connecting the ear to the brain, which can press on the trigeminal nerve
  \item \textbf{Migraine:} Some migraines cause numbness or tingling on one side of the face before or during the headache
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Numbness comes on suddenly, especially on one side of the face (possible stroke)
  \item You also have arm or leg weakness, slurred speech, or confusion
  \item The numbness follows a head injury
  \item You have trouble closing your eye or your face droops
  \item The numbness spreads or gets worse over time
  \item You have a rash on your face along with the numbness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Tingling or prickling sensation on the face
  \item Facial droop or weakness
  \item Difficulty closing one eye
  \item Drooling on one side of the mouth
  \item Changes in taste
  \item Eye pain or vision changes
  \item Headache
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} Sudden facial numbness with arm weakness or speech difficulty can be a stroke — call emergency services immediately.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Check your face in a mirror to see if the numbness is visible (drooping, uneven smile).
  \item Be careful when eating or drinking — you may not feel food or liquid on the affected side.
  \item Avoid hot foods or beverages that could burn your mouth without you noticing.
  \item Protect your face from extreme cold or heat that could cause injury without sensation.
  \item If your eye does not close fully, use artificial tears to prevent dryness and corneal injury.
  \item Seek immediate medical attention if numbness comes on suddenly, especially with weakness or speech difficulty.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Your doctor will ask when the numbness started, whether it comes and goes, and if you have other symptoms like weakness or pain.
  \item They will test sensation on different parts of your face and check your facial muscle strength.
  \item They will examine your eyes, mouth, and ears for signs of infection or other causes.
  \item Depending on the findings, they may order an MRI of the brain to look for nerve compression, stroke, or multiple sclerosis.
  \item Blood tests can check for infections, autoimmune conditions, or vitamin deficiencies.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Treatment depends on the underlying cause — Bell's palsy often improves on its own with supportive care.
  \item Steroids can reduce inflammation in conditions like Bell's palsy or multiple sclerosis flare-ups.
  \item Antiviral medications may help if shingles is the cause.
  \item Surgery may be needed for tumors that compress the nerve.
  \item Stroke requires emergency treatment to restore blood flow to the brain.
  \item Physical therapy can help with facial muscle weakness and coordination.
\end{itemize}

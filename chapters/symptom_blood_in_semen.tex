\chapter{Blood in Semen (Hematospermia)}

\section*{Definition}
Presence of blood in the ejaculate, presenting as pink, red, or brown-tinged semen, most often benign and self-limiting but requiring careful evaluation.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Bleeding typically arises from the prostate (most common), seminal vesicles, vas deferens, or urethra. In men under 40, infection or iatrogenic causes predominate; in men over 40, prostate pathology including malignancy must be excluded. Most cases are idiopathic with spontaneous resolution.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Seminal vesicle or prostate infection (prostatitis, seminal vesiculitis)
 \item Post-prostate biopsy (most common iatrogenic cause)
 \item Urethral stricture or trauma
 \item Prostate calculi
 \item Sexually transmitted infections
 \item Prostate or seminal vesicle cysts
 \item Hypertension or coagulopathy
 \item Prostate cancer (especially in men over 40)
 \item Testicular or prostate trauma
 \item Schistosomiasis (in endemic areas)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
 \item Persistent blood in semen beyond 3-4 weeks, recurrent episodes, or men over 40 with associated urinary symptoms
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item pain when urinating
 \item Urinary frequency
 \item Perineal pain
 \item Fever
 \item Lower back pain
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} Your doctor will check for serious underlying conditions when evaluating persistent blood in semen (hematospermia).

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Reassure yourself that most cases of blood in semen are benign and self-limiting, especially in men under 40.
 \item Note the colour (pink, red, dark brown) and duration of the bloody ejaculate to report to your doctor.
 \item Abstain from ejaculation for 24–48 hours if the bleeding is recurrent to allow the seminal tract to rest.
 \item Avoid vigorous sexual activity or trauma to the perineum and genitals until evaluated by a healthcare provider.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Your doctor will take a history including age, duration, frequency of episodes, recent genitourinary procedures, and associated urinary symptoms.
 \item Digital rectal examination assesses the prostate for nodules, tenderness, enlargement, or irregularity.
 \item Urinalysis and urine culture are performed to exclude urinary tract infection or haematuria; PSA blood test is considered in men over 40.
 \item Transrectal ultrasound of the prostate and seminal vesicles or MRI of the pelvis is arranged for persistent or recurrent haematospermia with no identified cause.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Observation and reassurance are the mainstays for idiopathic haematospermia — spontaneous resolution occurs within weeks in most cases.
 \item Antibiotic therapy (fluoroquinolone or doxycycline) is prescribed for infectious causes such as prostatitis or seminal vesiculitis.
 \item Transurethral resection is performed for obstructing prostatic cysts or calculi identified on imaging.
 \item In men over 40 with persistent haematospermia, further evaluation with cystoscopy and prostate biopsy may be indicated to exclude malignancy.
 \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
 \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

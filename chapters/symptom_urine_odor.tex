\chapter{Urine Odor}

\section*{Definition}
Unusual or strong smell of urine, which may be sweet, foul, fishy, or ammonia-like, reflecting dietary, metabolic, infectious, or medication-related causes.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Normal urine has a mild odor from byproducts of metabolism. Strong ammonia odor results from bacterial urea breakdown (UTI or prolonged urine stasis). Sweet or fruity odor suggests ketones (diabetic ketoacidosis, starvation) or maple syrup urine disease. Fishy odor indicates bacterial vaginosis or trichomonas (contaminating urine sample) or trimethylaminuria. Asparagus causes a characteristic sulfurous odor from asparagusic acid metabolism.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Urinary tract infection (ammonia or foul odor)
  \item Dehydration (concentrated urine with strong ammonia)
  \item Dietary: asparagus, garlic, onions, curry, coffee
  \item Diabetic ketoacidosis (sweet or fruity odor)
  \item Medications and supplements (B vitamins, sulfonamides)
  \item Liver failure (musty odor)
  \item Maple syrup urine disease (sweet, maple syrup odor in infants)
  \item Phenylketonuria (musty or mousy odor)
  \item Trimethylaminuria (fishy odor, rare metabolic disorder)
  \item Bacterial vaginosis or trichomonas (contaminating urine sample)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Urine odor with fever, flank pain, painful urination, blood in urine, or unusual odor persisting without clear dietary cause
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Frequent urination
  \item Painful urination
  \item Urinary urgency
  \item Dehydration
  \item Vaginal discharge
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Increase water intake to two to three litres daily to dilute urine concentration and reduce ammonia-like odour from dehydration.
  \item Identify dietary triggers: asparagus, garlic, onion, curry, coffee, and cruciferous vegetables cause temporary odour changes and are harmless.
  \item Maintain good perineal hygiene; for suspected odour from vaginal contamination, use a clean-catch midstream collection technique for accurate assessment.
  \item Avoid heavily marketed vaginal odour washes and douches, which alter the normal flora and can worsen the underlying issue.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Urinalysis with microscopy and dipstick: assess for leukocyte esterase, nitrites, pH, ketones, and glucose; urine culture and sensitivity if infection is suspected.
  \item If sweet or fruity odour is present: point-of-care glucose and serum ketones or beta-hydroxybutyrate to screen for diabetic ketoacidosis.
  \item If UTI is ruled out and unusual odour persists: metabolic workup for inborn errors of metabolism (phenylketonuria, maple syrup urine disease, trimethylaminuria) in the appropriate clinical context.
  \item For fishy odour: vaginal pH, KOH whiff test, and wet mount for clue cells (bacterial vaginosis) or motile trichomonads.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item If UTI is confirmed: targeted antibiotic therapy based on culture and sensitivity (nitrofurantoin 100 mg BID or TMP-SMX 160/800 mg BID for three to five days for uncomplicated cystitis).
  \item Dehydration-associated odour: education on adequate daily water intake; treat underlying causes of polyuria (poorly controlled diabetes, diuretic use).
  \item If bacterial vaginosis is the contaminant: metronidazole gel 0.75\% once daily intravaginally for five days or oral metronidazole 500 mg BID for seven days.
  \item Metabolic disorders: referral to a metabolic disease specialist; dietary modifications (low protein for PKU, low choline for trimethylaminuria); no pharmaceutical cure for these genetic disorders.
\end{itemize}

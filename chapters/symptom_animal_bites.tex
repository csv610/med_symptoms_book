\chapter{Animal Bites}

\section*{Definition}
Animal bites are wounds caused by the teeth of animals. Dog bites are most common, followed by cat bites, which have a higher infection risk. Bites can cause puncture wounds, lacerations, crush injuries, and infections.

\section*{Pathophysiology}
Animal teeth introduce bacteria deep into tissues. Cat bites have small, deep puncture wounds that inoculate bacteria into joint capsules and tendon sheaths. Dog bites cause more crush and tissue damage. Common pathogens include Pasteurella multocida (cats and dogs), Staphylococcus, Streptococcus, and anaerobes. Rabies is a rare but fatal viral infection transmitted through saliva.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Dog bites (most common, 80-90 percent of animal bites)
\item Cat bites (higher infection risk)
\item Human bites (highest infection risk, risk of hepatitis B/C, HIV)
\item Rodent bites (rats, mice, hamsters)
\item Wild animal bites (raccoons, bats, foxes - rabies risk)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Puncture wound, laceration, or crush injury
\item Pain, swelling, and redness at the bite site
\item Signs of infection: purulent drainage, warmth, spreading erythema
\item Fever
\item Loss of function or sensation
\item Deep wounds involving joints, tendons, or bones
\item History of the biting animal (unknown, stray, or wild)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Cellulitis (wound infection)
\item Abscess formation
\item Tetanus
\item Rabies
\item Osteomyelitis (bone infection)
\item Septic arthritis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care/Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Wash the wound thoroughly with soap and water for 15 minutes
\item Apply direct pressure if bleeding
\item Elevate the wound
\item Apply antibiotic ointment and cover with a clean bandage
\item Seek medical evaluation for all but the most trivial bites
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clinical assessment of wound depth, location, and contamination
\item Wound culture if infection is present
\item X-ray to rule out foreign body or fracture
\item Blood tests for systemic infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Wound irrigation and debridement
\item Primary closure for cosmetic areas (face) or delayed closure for contaminated wounds
\item Prophylactic antibiotics for high-risk bites (cat, human, hand wounds)
\item Rabies postexposure prophylaxis if indicated
\item Tetanus prophylaxis
\item Elevation and wound care follow-up
\end{itemize}

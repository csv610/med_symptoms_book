\chapter{Allergic Reaction}

\section{Definition}
An allergic reaction is an immune system response to a normally harmless substance (allergen). Reactions range from mild (sneezing, hives) to severe and life-threatening (anaphylaxis). Immediate hypersensitivity (type I) is IgE-mediated.

\section{Pathophysiology}
Allergic reactions involve sensitization and re-exposure. During first exposure, allergen-specific IgE antibodies bind to mast cells and basophils. On re-exposure, the allergen cross-links IgE, triggering mast cell degranulation and release of histamine, leukotrienes, and prostaglandins. These mediators cause vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, bronchoconstriction, and mucus secretion.

\section{Etiology}
\begin{itemize}
\item Foods: peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish, eggs, milk, wheat, soy
\item Medications: penicillins, cephalosporins, sulfonamides, NSAIDs, aspirin
\item Insect stings: bees, wasps, hornets, fire ants
\item Environmental: pollen, dust mites, mold, pet dander
\item Latex
\item Contact: poison ivy, nickel, fragrances
\end{itemize}

\section{Indications for Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
\item Skin: hives (urticaria), redness, itching, angioedema (swelling of lips, tongue, eyelids)
\item Respiratory: sneezing, nasal congestion, wheezing, shortness of breath, throat tightness
\item Gastrointestinal: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea
\item Cardiovascular: dizziness, hypotension, tachycardia, syncope
\item Severe: difficulty breathing, stridor, hoarseness, loss of consciousness (anaphylaxis)
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Anaphylaxis (life-threatening)
\item Urticaria (hives)
\item Angioedema
\item Allergic rhinitis (hay fever)
\item Asthma
\item Atopic dermatitis (eczema)
\item Contact dermatitis
\end{itemize}

\section{Self-Care/Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Identify and avoid known allergens
\item Carry an epinephrine auto-injector if at risk for anaphylaxis
\item Take OTC antihistamines for mild reactions
\item Wear medical alert identification
\item Inform healthcare providers of all allergies
\end{itemize}

\section{Diagnostic Approach}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clinical history of reaction to specific triggers
\item Skin prick testing
\item Specific IgE blood tests (ImmunoCAP)
\item Oral food challenge in controlled setting
\end{itemize}

\section{Treatment/Management Overview}
\begin{itemize}
\item Epinephrine intramuscular is first-line for anaphylaxis
\item Antihistamines (diphenhydramine, cetirizine) for mild reactions
\item Corticosteroids for delayed reactions
\item Bronchodilators for respiratory symptoms
\item Allergen immunotherapy (allergy shots) for selected cases
\end{itemize}

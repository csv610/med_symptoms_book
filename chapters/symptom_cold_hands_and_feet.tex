\chapter{Cold Hands and Feet (Raynaud Phenomenon)}

\section*{Definition}
A vasospastic disorder characterized by episodic digital ischemia triggered by cold or stress, causing well-demarcated color changes (white, blue, red) in the fingers and/or toes, often with numbness, tingling, and pain during rewarming.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Raynaud phenomenon involves exaggerated narrowing of blood vessels of digital arteries and arterioles in response to cold or emotional stress. Primary Raynaud is idiopathic, benign, and associated with hyperactivation of alpha-2 adrenergic receptors on digital vessels. Secondary Raynaud involves underlying pathology (scleroderma, lupus) causing structural vessel damage, intimal hyperplasia, and obliterative microvascular disease, leading to more severe ischemia and potential digital ulceration.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Primary Raynaud disease (benign, no underlying cause, onset before age 30)
  \item Connective tissue diseases (scleroderma, systemic lupus, mixed connective tissue disease)
  \item Cryoglobulinemia (abnormal cold-precipitating proteins)
  \item Buerger disease (thromboangiitis obliterans, linked to smoking)
  \item Atherosclerosis or peripheral artery disease
  \item Thoracic outlet syndrome (neurovascular compression)
  \item Medications (beta-blockers, ergotamines, chemotherapy bleomycin, cisplatin)
  \item Repetitive vibration injury (hand-arm vibration syndrome from power tools)
  \item Hypothyroidism (reduced metabolic rate, cold intolerance)
  \item Carpal tunnel syndrome (associated with vasomotor symptoms)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Unilateral symptoms, skin ulceration or gangrene, symptoms beginning after age 30, or associated with rash, joint pain, or systemic symptoms
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Numbness
  \item Tingling
  \item Finger swelling
  \item Joint pain
  \item Digital ulcers
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Wear warm gloves, socks, and layers in cold environments and use hand warmers or thermal packs when going outdoors.
  \item Avoid triggers including smoking (nicotine causes vasoconstriction), caffeine, and sudden temperature changes.
  \item Practise stress reduction techniques (deep breathing, meditation) as emotional stress can trigger vasospastic episodes.
  \item Perform gentle circular arm swinging or warm-water hand and foot soaks to promote vasodilation and symptom relief during an attack.
  \item Protect fingers and toes from cuts, scrapes, and infections as healing is impaired in vasospastic digits.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Your doctor will distinguish primary from secondary Raynaud by age of onset, symmetry, severity, and presence of digital ulcers or systemic symptoms.
  \item Nailfold capillaroscopy is performed at the bedside to identify dilated or giant capillaries and avascular areas suggestive of an underlying connective tissue disease.
  \item Laboratory testing includes antinuclear antibody (ANA), erythrocyte sedimentation rate, and specific autoantibodies (anti-Scl-70, anti-centromere) if scleroderma is suspected.
  \item Referral to a rheumatologist is arranged for suspected secondary Raynaud, especially in the presence of positive autoantibodies or abnormal nailfold capillaries.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Calcium channel blockers (nifedipine, amlodipine) are first-line pharmacotherapy for Raynaud phenomenon, reducing frequency and severity of attacks.
  \item Topical nitroglycerin ointment applied to the affected digits may be used for acute vasospastic episodes.
  \item For severe secondary Raynaud, additional therapies include phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (sildenafil), endothelin receptor antagonists (bosentan), or intravenous prostacyclin analogues.
  \item Digital sympathectomy is considered for refractory cases with threatened digit loss, and botulinum toxin injections have shown benefit in some patients with severe Raynaud.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

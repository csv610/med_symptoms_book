\chapter{Sore Throat}

\section*{Definition}
A sore throat is pain, scratchiness, or irritation in your throat that often gets worse when you swallow or talk.

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Seek urgent care for difficulty breathing, inability to swallow fluids, drooling, severe dehydration, or rapidly worsening symptoms.
  \item Arrange medical assessment for persistent or severe pain, high fever, rash, neck swelling, or suspected strep infection.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How It Develops}
Most sore throats are caused by viral infections, but bacterial causes like strep throat need antibiotic treatment. Other causes include post-nasal drip, reflux, smoking, or dry air.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Viral URI
  \item Streptococcal pharyngitis
  \item Allergies
  \item Dry air/smoking
  \item Acid reflux
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fever
  \item Swollen glands
  \item Tonsillar exudate
  \item Voice change
  \item Difficulty swallowing
\end{itemize}

\section*{Medical Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Examination assesses the throat, tonsils, neck nodes, hydration, breathing, and signs of an abscess or airway problem.
  \item A rapid strep test or throat culture may be used when bacterial pharyngitis is suspected; imaging is reserved for selected complications.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Most viral sore throats improve with supportive care; confirmed or strongly suspected streptococcal infection may require clinician-prescribed antibiotics.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care and Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Drink fluids, rest, avoid smoke, and use warm liquids, throat lozenges, or honey for adults and children over 1 year.
  \item Use acetaminophen or ibuprofen only if safe and according to the label; do not give aspirin to children.
\end{itemize}

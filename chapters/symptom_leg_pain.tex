\chapter{Leg Pain}

\section*{Definition}
Pain or discomfort anywhere in the lower extremity from the hip to the foot, arising from musculoskeletal, vascular, neurological, or dermatological sources.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Leg pain mechanisms include nociceptive (musculoskeletal injury, inflammation), neuropathic (nerve compression or damage from disc herniation, peripheral neuropathy), ischemic (peripheral arterial disease, DVT), and referred (hip or spine pathology presenting as thigh or leg pain).

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Peripheral arterial disease (claudication)
  \item Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)
  \item Sciatica or lumbar radiculopathy
  \item Muscle strain or tear (hamstring, quadriceps, calf)
  \item Peripheral neuropathy (diabetic, alcoholic)
  \item Varicose veins or chronic venous insufficiency
  \item Compartment syndrome (acute or chronic)
  \item Shin splints (medial tibial stress syndrome)
  \item Stress fracture
  \item Cellulitis or soft tissue infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Sudden severe leg pain with swelling and redness (possible DVT), leg pain with chest pain or shortness of breath, or non-healing ulcers
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Swelling
  \item Numbness or tingling
  \item Skin changes
  \item Ulcers
  \item Calf tenderness
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Elevate the leg and apply ice packs for 15-20 minutes to reduce swelling and pain from recent injuries or venous insufficiency.
  \item Wear compression stockings (graduated, medical grade) if leg pain is related to varicose veins or chronic venous insufficiency — consult a pharmacist for proper sizing.
  \item Walk or perform gentle calf-pumping exercises regularly to promote venous return and reduce aching from venous stasis.
  \item Avoid prolonged sitting or standing; take breaks to move and stretch every 30-60 minutes during the day.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Vascular examination including palpation of peripheral pulses (femoral, popliteal, dorsalis pedis, posterior tibial), ankle-brachial index (ABI), and inspection for swelling, skin changes, and ulcers.
  \item Duplex ultrasound of the lower extremity is indicated to rule out DVT (with compression ultrasound) or evaluate venous insufficiency.
  \item Neurological examination including sensory, motor, and reflex assessment to identify radiculopathy or peripheral neuropathy.
  \item CT angiography or MR angiography is reserved for suspected peripheral arterial disease with abnormal ABI or critical limb ischaemia.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Deep vein thrombosis requires immediate anticoagulation (heparin transitioned to warfarin or DOAC) for a minimum of 3-6 months.
  \item Peripheral arterial disease is managed with cardiovascular risk reduction (statin, antiplatelet, smoking cessation) and supervised exercise therapy.
  \item Venous insufficiency and varicose veins are treated with graduated compression stockings, leg elevation, and endovenous ablation or sclerotherapy for symptomatic veins.
  \item Muscular or radicular leg pain benefits from physical therapy, NSAIDs, and treatment of the underlying spinal pathology when present.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

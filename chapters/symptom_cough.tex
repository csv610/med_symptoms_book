\chapter{Cough}

\section*{Definition}
A cough is a reflex that helps clear the airways of mucus, particles, or irritants. It is often described as acute (up to 3 weeks), subacute (3--8 weeks), or chronic (more than 8 weeks in adults).

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Seek urgent care for difficulty breathing, blue lips, coughing blood, chest pain, confusion, or a severe rapidly worsening cough.
  \item Arrange assessment for a cough lasting more than 3 weeks, recurrent cough, or cough with fever, weight loss, night sweats, or low oxygen.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How It Develops}
The cough reflex is activated by irritation or inflammation in the airways, throat, or sometimes the upper gastrointestinal tract. Its timing, duration, triggers, and associated symptoms help identify the cause.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Viral URI
  \item Asthma/COPD
  \item GERD
  \item Postnasal drip
  \item Smoking/smoke irritation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Sore throat
  \item Congestion
  \item Wheezing
  \item Chest tightness
  \item Phlegm production
\end{itemize}

\section*{Medical Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item History focuses on onset, duration, character of symptoms (productive vs dry cough, nasal discharge color), fever pattern, and exposure history.
  \item Physical examination includes vital signs, lung auscultation, nasal inspection, and oropharyngeal examination.
  \item Testing is guided by the clinical pattern. Chest imaging or respiratory-virus testing may be useful in selected acute illnesses, while spirometry is commonly considered for persistent cough or suspected asthma or COPD.
  \item Allergy evaluation, reflux assessment, sputum testing, or specialist referral is reserved for selected cases.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Treatment depends on the cause; uncomplicated viral cough is usually managed with fluids, rest, and symptom relief.
  \item Antibiotics are not routinely useful for viral cough and should be prescribed only when a bacterial cause is suspected or confirmed.
  \item Asthma, COPD, reflux, postnasal-drip, and chronic cough require cause-specific treatment and assessment when persistent.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care and Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Maintain adequate hydration with water, warm tea, or broth to thin secretions.
  \item A clean humidifier may soothe irritation; avoid very hot steam because of burn risk.
  \item Rest and avoid exposure to smoke, dust, and other respiratory irritants.
  \item Over-the-counter remedies such as saline nasal spray, cough drops, or honey (for adults) may provide symptomatic relief.
  \item Monitor for warning signs including high fever, difficulty breathing, chest pain, or worsening symptoms.
\end{itemize}

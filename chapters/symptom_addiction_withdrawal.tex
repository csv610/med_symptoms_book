\chapter{Addiction Withdrawal}

\section*{Definition}
A syndrome of physical and psychological symptoms that occurs when a person stops or reduces use of a substance to which they have developed dependence.

\section*{Pathophysiology}
Chronic substance use causes neuroadaptation — your brain compensates for the drug's effects by altering neurotransmitter systems (dopamine, GABA, glutamate, norepinephrine, opioid). Abrupt cessation removes the drug effect, unmasking the compensatory changes causing rebound hyperactivity or hypoactivity depending on the substance.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Alcohol withdrawal (potentially severe — DTs, seizures, death)
 \item Opioid withdrawal (flu-like symptoms, cravings, not life-threatening)
 \item Benzodiazepine withdrawal (prolonged, can cause seizures)
 \item Nicotine withdrawal (cigarettes, vaping)
 \item Stimulant withdrawal (amphetamine, cocaine — fatigue, depression)
 \item Cannabis withdrawal (irritability, insomnia, anxiety)
 \item Antidepressant discontinuation syndrome
 \item Caffeine withdrawal (headache, fatigue, irritability)
 \item Antipsychotic withdrawal
 \item Barbiturate withdrawal (severe, can be life-threatening)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
 \item Severe or worsening symptoms
 \item Severe withdrawal symptoms, history of heavy alcohol/benzodiazepine use, suicidal thoughts, or hallucinations
 \item Accompanied by other concerning signs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Anxiety
 \item Insomnia
 \item Agitation
 \item Sweating
 \item Palpitations
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} Your doctor will check for serious underlying conditions when evaluating persistent addiction withdrawal.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Rest and avoid activities that make it worse.
 \item Maintain adequate hydration and a balanced diet.
 \item Over-the-counter remedies may provide symptomatic relief (consult a pharmacist or doctor).
 \item Monitor symptoms and seek professional advice if they persist or worsen.
 \item Avoid self-medicating with prescription medications without medical guidance.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item A thorough history and physical examination are the first steps in evaluation.
 \item Laboratory tests (blood work, urinalysis) may be ordered based on clinical suspicion.
 \item Imaging studies (X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI) may be indicated when structural pathology is suspected.
 \item Specialist referral is arranged when the diagnosis remains uncertain or requires specific expertise.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Treatment targets the underlying cause identified through diagnostic evaluation.
 \item Supportive care and symptom management are provided while awaiting definitive therapy.
 \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
 \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

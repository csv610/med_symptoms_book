\chapter{Addiction Withdrawal}

\section*{Definition}
A syndrome of physical and psychological symptoms that occurs when a person stops or reduces use of a substance to which they have developed dependence.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Chronic substance use causes neuroadaptation — your brain compensates for the drug's effects by altering neurotransmitter systems (dopamine, GABA, glutamate, norepinephrine, opioid). Abrupt cessation removes the drug effect, unmasking the compensatory changes causing rebound hyperactivity or hypoactivity depending on the substance.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Alcohol withdrawal (potentially severe — DTs, seizures, death)
 \item Opioid withdrawal (flu-like symptoms, cravings, not life-threatening)
 \item Benzodiazepine withdrawal (prolonged, can cause seizures)
 \item Nicotine withdrawal (cigarettes, vaping)
 \item Stimulant withdrawal (amphetamine, cocaine — fatigue, depression)
 \item Cannabis withdrawal (irritability, insomnia, anxiety)
 \item Antidepressant discontinuation syndrome
 \item Caffeine withdrawal (headache, fatigue, irritability)
 \item Antipsychotic withdrawal
 \item Barbiturate withdrawal (severe, can be life-threatening)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
 \item Severe withdrawal symptoms, history of heavy alcohol/benzodiazepine use, suicidal thoughts, or hallucinations
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Anxiety
 \item Insomnia
 \item Agitation
 \item Sweating
 \item Palpitations
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} Your doctor will check for serious underlying conditions when evaluating persistent addiction withdrawal.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Stay hydrated with water and electrolyte-containing fluids to manage fluid losses from sweating, vomiting, or diarrhoea.
 \item Eat small, frequent meals of bland, easily tolerated foods and avoid caffeine and sugar which can worsen anxiety and tremors.
 \item Engage supportive family or friends to maintain accountability; consider attending peer support groups (AA, NA, SMART Recovery).
 \item Contact a healthcare provider or addiction service before attempting withdrawal from alcohol or benzodiazepines, as these can cause life-threatening seizures without medical supervision.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Your doctor will assess the type, amount, duration, and timing of the last use of the substance, and screen for polysubstance use.
 \item Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment (CIWA-Ar) for alcohol or COWS for opioids will be used to objectively grade withdrawal severity.
 \item Laboratory studies including complete blood count, electrolytes, liver function tests, and urine toxicology screening will be performed.
 \item Referral to an addiction medicine specialist or medically supervised detoxification programme is arranged for moderate-to-severe withdrawal.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Medically supervised detoxification using tapering protocols or substitution therapy (benzodiazepines for alcohol, methadone/buprenorphine for opioids) is the standard of care.
 \item Symptom-triggered or fixed-schedule pharmacotherapy is administered based on withdrawal severity scores.
 \item Long-term relapse prevention includes maintenance pharmacotherapy (naltrexone, acamprosate, buprenorphine) combined with behavioural therapy.
 \item Treatment of co-occurring psychiatric conditions (depression, anxiety) is essential for sustained recovery.
 \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
 \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

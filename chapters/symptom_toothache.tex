\chapter{Toothache (Dental Pain)}

\section*{Definition}
Pain originating from a tooth or its supporting structures, ranging from mild sensitivity to severe throbbing pain, caused by dental caries, infection, trauma, or dental procedures.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Dental caries (decay) progresses through enamel into dentin, exposing sensitive nerve endings. Pulpitis (inflammation of dental pulp) results from deep decay, trauma, or thermal injury. Periapical collection of pus forms when pulp infection extends through the apical foramen into surrounding bone. Periodontal collection of pus arises from gum infection in periodontal pockets.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Dental caries (tooth decay) — most common cause
  \item Pulpitis (reversible or irreversible)
  \item Periapical collection of pus (dental collection of pus)
  \item Periodontal collection of pus (gum infection)
  \item Cracked tooth syndrome
  \item Dental trauma (fractured or avulsed tooth)
  \item Impacted wisdom teeth (pericoronitis)
  \item Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) disorders (referred pain)
  \item Sinusitis (referred pain to upper teeth)
  \item Dental procedures (recent filling, root canal, extraction)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Severe tooth pain with facial swelling, fever, difficulty swallowing or breathing (possible deep space neck infection), or persistent pain beyond 2 days
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Facial swelling
  \item Fever
  \item Headache
  \item Gum swelling
  \item Earache (referred)
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Rinse the mouth with warm salt water several times daily to dislodge debris and reduce gum inflammation.
  \item Take ibuprofen 400--600 mg or acetaminophen 500--1000 mg every six hours as needed for pain; avoid placing aspirin directly on the gum as it causes chemical burns.
  \item Apply a cold compress to the cheek over the painful area for 15 minutes at a time to reduce swelling from infection or trauma.
  \item Avoid very hot, cold, or sweet foods and liquids that may provoke sharp pain from exposed dentin or pulpitis; chew on the unaffected side.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Dental examination: inspection for caries, periodontal pockets, gingival swelling, and sinus tract; percussion testing (pain on tapping suggests periapical pathology); cold and heat sensitivity testing.
  \item Dental radiographs: periapical X-ray to evaluate caries depth, periapical lucency, and root fractures; panoramic radiograph for impacted molars or sinus pathology.
  \item If facial swelling, fever, or trismus is present: CT maxillofacial to assess for deep space infection (submandibular, sublingual, or buccal space abscess).
  \item Referral to endodontist for root canal therapy if irreversible pulpitis or periapical abscess is confirmed; to oral surgery for extraction or complicated impactions.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Reversible pulpitis: remove caries and place a sedative temporary filling; avoid extreme temperatures; definitive restoration at follow-up.
  \item Irreversible pulpitis or periapical abscess: root canal therapy (cleaning and obturation of the canal system) or extraction for non-restorable teeth.
  \item Periodontal abscess: incisional drainage, scaling and root planing of the periodontal pocket, and systemic antibiotics (amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily for seven days) if spreading cellulitis is present.
  \item Pericoronitis (impacted wisdom tooth): irrigation under the operculum, chlorhexidine 0.12\% mouthwash twice daily, amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily for five days, and extraction of the offending tooth after infection resolves.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Green Stool}

\section*{Definition}
Green-colored stool resulting from the presence of bile pigments, rapid intestinal transit, or ingestion of green-colored foods or supplements.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Bilirubin produced from heme breakdown is conjugated in the liver, secreted into bile, and normally metabolized by gut bacteria to stercobilin (brown pigment). Green stool occurs when bile passes through the intestine too rapidly for bacterial conversion to stercobilin, or when biliverdin (green pigment) is not fully reduced. Dietary chlorophyll (green vegetables) and artificial food coloring (green dyes) are common benign causes.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Dietary: green leafy vegetables (spinach, kale)
  \item Food coloring (green dyes in beverages, candies, gelatin)
  \item Rapid intestinal transit (diarrhea, IBS-D)
  \item Bile acid malabsorption
  \item Antibiotic use (altered gut flora)
  \item Iron supplements
  \item Green-colored medications or supplements
  \item Infectious gastroenteritis (especially Salmonella)
  \item Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis
  \item Malabsorption syndromes
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Persistent green stool with diarrhea, weight loss, abdominal pain, or other concerning GI symptoms
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Diarrhea
  \item Abdominal pain
  \item Bloating
  \item Weight loss
  \item Nausea
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Review recent diet — green vegetables, food coloring, and supplements are common benign causes.
  \item Ensure adequate hydration, especially if green stool is associated with diarrhea.
  \item Keep a food and medication diary to identify triggers.
  \item Consider whether antibiotics or iron supplements may be contributing.
  \item Seek medical advice if green stool persists or is accompanied by other concerning symptoms.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Your doctor will take a dietary history and review medications that may alter stool color.
  \item Stool studies may be ordered to check for infection, inflammation, or malabsorption.
  \item Blood tests assess for anemia, nutritional deficiencies, and inflammatory markers.
  \item Colonoscopy or upper endoscopy may be indicated for persistent green stool with GI symptoms.
  \item Referral to a gastroenterologist for chronic or unexplained cases.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Reassurance and dietary modification if a benign dietary cause is identified.
  \item Treatment of underlying infection (antibiotics for bacterial gastroenteritis) if indicated.
  \item Bile acid sequestrants (cholestyramine) for bile acid malabsorption.
  \item Management of IBS-D with dietary changes, antispasmodics, or antidiarrheals.
  \item Treatment of inflammatory bowel disease with appropriate anti-inflammatory medications.
\end{itemize}

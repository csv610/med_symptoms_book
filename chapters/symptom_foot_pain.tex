\chapter{Foot Pain}

\section*{Definition}
Pain or discomfort localized anywhere in the foot, from the hindfoot (heel, ankle) through the midfoot (arch) to the forefoot (toes, metatarsals), with causes ranging from mechanical to systemic.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Foot pain causes varies by location: heel pain most commonly from plantar fasciitis; midfoot pain from degenerative arthritis or stress fracture; metatarsalgia (forefoot pain) from weight-bearing overload, Morton neuroma, or Freiberg infarction; great toe pain from hallux valgus (bunion), hallux rigidus, or gout. The foot's 26 bones, 33 joints, and complex ligamentous/tendinous architecture create numerous pain generators.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Plantar fasciitis
  \item Metatarsalgia
  \item Morton neuroma (interdigital neuroma)
  \item Hallux valgus (bunion)
  \item Hallux rigidus (great toe arthritis)
  \item Hammer toe or claw toe deformity
  \item Stress fracture (metatarsal, navicular)
  \item Tarsal tunnel syndrome
  \item Gout (usually first metatarsophalangeal joint)
  \item Flatfoot (pes planus) or cavus foot deformity
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Foot pain with inability to bear weight, open wound or ulcer, fever, redness spreading up the leg, or sudden severe pain with swelling
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Heel pain
  \item Ankle pain
  \item Toe pain
  \item Swelling
  \item Numbness or tingling
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Rest and elevate the foot to reduce swelling and pain.
  \item Apply ice packs to the painful area for 15-20 minutes, several times daily.
  \item Wear supportive, well-cushioned shoes with proper arch support.
  \item Use over-the-counter arch supports or orthotic inserts for mechanical foot pain.
  \item Stretch the plantar fascia and Achilles tendon gently, especially before standing.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Your doctor will examine your foot for tenderness, swelling, deformity, and range of motion.
  \item Gait analysis and assessment of footwear may provide clues to the underlying cause.
  \item Weight-bearing X-rays are the first-line imaging for most foot pain causes.
  \item Ultrasound or MRI may be ordered for soft tissue evaluation (plantar fascia, tendons, Morton neuroma).
  \item Blood tests (uric acid, inflammatory markers) are performed if gout or inflammatory arthritis is suspected.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Physical therapy with stretching and strengthening exercises for plantar fasciitis and tendinopathy.
  \item Custom orthotics or supportive footwear for biomechanical issues (flat feet, high arches).
  \item NSAIDs or corticosteroid injections for inflammatory conditions.
  \item Surgical options (plantar fascia release, bunionectomy, neuroma excision) for refractory cases.
  \item Urate-lowering therapy (allopurinol) for gout and disease-modifying drugs for inflammatory arthritis.
\end{itemize}

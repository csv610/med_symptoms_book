\chapter{Rectal Pain (Proctalgia)}

\section*{Definition}
Rectal pain is discomfort in the anal canal or rectum. It can range from sharp, brief episodes (proctalgia fugax) to chronic pain associated with underlying conditions such as hemorrhoids, anal fissures, or abscesses.

\section*{Pathophysiology}
The anal canal is rich in sensory nerve endings, making it highly sensitive to pain. Causes include: stretching of the internal anal sphincter (fissure), inflammation or thrombosis of hemorrhoids, infection (abscess), muscle spasm (proctalgia fugax), or inflammation of the rectal lining (proctitis). The pelvic floor muscles can also contribute to pain.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Anal fissure (tear in the anal mucosa)
\item Hemorrhoids (thrombosed or inflamed)
\item Anorectal abscess
\item Anal fistula
\item Proctalgia fugax (brief, idiopathic sphincter spasm)
\item Levator ani syndrome (chronic pelvic floor tension)
\item Proctitis (inflammatory bowel disease, infection, radiation)
\item Constipation or diarrhea (irritation)
\item Foreign body or trauma
\item Coccydynia (tailbone pain)
\item Pudendal neuralgia
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Sharp or burning pain in the anal or rectal area
\item Pain during bowel movements (defecation)
\item Pain that radiates to the lower back, buttocks, or perineum
\item Bleeding with bowel movements
\item Sensation of a lump or mass
\item Discharge or pus from the anus
\item Itching or irritation
\item Pain that awakens from sleep (proctalgia fugax)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rectal bleeding
\item Hemorrhoids
\item Anal fissure
\item Anal itching (pruritus ani)
\item Constipation
\item Inflammatory bowel disease
\item Proctitis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care/Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Sitz baths (warm water soaks) for 15-20 minutes several times a day
\item Apply OTC hemorrhoid creams or suppositories
\item Take stool softeners to prevent straining
\item Drink plenty of water and eat a high-fiber diet
\item Avoid prolonged sitting on hard surfaces
\item Apply cold packs for acute swelling
\item Use a cushion or donut pillow when sitting
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Digital rectal examination
\item Anoscopy
\item Proctoscopy or sigmoidoscopy for higher lesions
\item Colonoscopy if bleeding or inflammatory bowel disease suspected
\item MRI or endoanal ultrasound for complex fistulas or abscesses
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Treat underlying cause (fissure, hemorrhoids, abscess)
\item Topical nitroglycerin or nifedipine for anal fissure
\item Hemorrhoidectomy or rubber band ligation for hemorrhoids
\item Incision and drainage for abscess
\item Fistulotomy for anal fistula
\item Muscle relaxants or biofeedback for levator ani syndrome
\item Nerve blocks for pudendal neuralgia
\end{itemize}

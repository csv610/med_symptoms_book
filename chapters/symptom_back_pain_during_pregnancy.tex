\chapter{Back Pain During Pregnancy}

\section*{Definition}
Back pain during pregnancy is pain in the lower, middle, or upper back occurring during gestation. Mechanical strain is common, but severe, new, radiating, fever-associated, or neurologic pain may signal infection, spinal disease, kidney disease, preterm labor, or another emergency.

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
Contact your obstetric care provider promptly for back pain with fever, flank pain, or painful urination; vaginal bleeding or fluid leakage; regular painful contractions; abdominal pain; or reduced fetal movement. Seek emergency care for leg weakness, saddle numbness, loss of bladder or bowel control, or sudden severe pain after trauma.

\section*{How It Develops}
Pregnancy changes posture, shifts the center of gravity, stretches and weakens the abdominal muscles, and loosens pelvic ligaments. These changes can increase strain on the back and pelvic joints. Kidney infection, kidney stones, nerve compression, uterine contractions, placental complications, and rare spinal conditions can produce more serious pain.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Postural strain, pelvic-girdle pain, sacroiliac dysfunction, or muscle spasm
  \item Sciatica or disk-related nerve-root irritation
  \item Urinary tract infection, kidney infection, or kidney stone
  \item Preterm labor, placental abruption, or other obstetric complication
  \item Fracture, inflammatory disease, or spinal cord/cauda equina compression
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pelvic pressure, contractions, abdominal pain, or vaginal bleeding
  \item Fever, chills, urinary symptoms, nausea, or flank pain
  \item Numbness, weakness, shooting leg pain, or difficulty walking
\end{itemize}

\section*{Medical Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item History includes gestational age, pain pattern, trauma, fever, urinary symptoms, neurologic symptoms, contractions, bleeding, and fetal movement.
  \item Examination assesses spine, gait, neurologic function, abdomen, costovertebral angle, uterine activity, and fetal status.
  \item Urinalysis, urine culture, blood tests, fetal monitoring, ultrasound, or MRI is selected according to the findings and gestational age. Necessary imaging should not be withheld; ultrasound and MRI are generally preferred when they can answer the clinical question, while CT or radiography may still be appropriate when needed.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
Treatment may include physical therapy, activity modification, pregnancy-safe analgesia, antibiotics, treatment of renal disease, or urgent obstetric or neurologic care. Acetaminophen/paracetamol may be considered after discussion with the obstetric clinician; avoid self-starting NSAIDs, especially from 20 weeks onward, unless specifically advised. Persistent disabling pain warrants reassessment rather than prolonged self-treatment.

\section*{Self-Care and Home Management}
For mild mechanical pain, use posture support, gentle activity, pregnancy-adapted stretching, and heat or cold applied safely to the back. Avoid prolonged bed rest and heavy lifting. Ask the prenatal clinician before using analgesics, braces, or exercises; stop exercise if it causes pain or if bleeding, fluid leakage, dizziness, chest pain, shortness of breath, weakness, or regular painful contractions occur.

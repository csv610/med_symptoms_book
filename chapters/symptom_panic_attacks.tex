\chapter{Panic Attacks}

\section*{Definition}
Sudden episodes of intense fear or discomfort that peak within minutes, accompanied by physical symptoms such as palpitations, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, chest pain, nausea, dizziness, and a sense of impending doom.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Panic attacks involve acute activation of the amygdala and its projections to the hypothalamus, periaqueductal grey, and locus coeruleus, triggering the sympathetic nervous system (fight-or-flight response). Oestrogen modulates the serotonergic and GABAergic systems that inhibit amygdala reactivity; declining oestrogen reduces inhibitory tone, lowering the panic threshold. Reduced progesterone (with its anxiolytic GABA-agonist metabolites) further disinhibits the panic circuit. Respiratory and interoceptive hypersensitivity (catastrophic misinterpretation of bodily sensations) amplifies the panic response.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Panic disorder (recurrent unexpected panic attacks with fear of recurrence)
  \item Perimenopausal and menopausal hormonal fluctuations
  \item Generalised anxiety disorder
  \item Major depressive disorder
  \item Post-traumatic stress disorder
  \item Hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis)
  \item Hypoglycaemia
  \item Mitral valve prolapse
  \item Substance use or withdrawal (caffeine, alcohol, cocaine, cannabis)
  \item Medication side effects (stimulants, thyroid hormone)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Recurrent panic attacks, panic attacks with suicidal thoughts, new onset after age 40 without clear trigger, or chest pain with shortness of breath (rule out cardiac)
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Anxiety
  \item Heart palpitations
  \item Shortness of breath
  \item Dizziness
  \item Chest pain
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Practise slow, controlled breathing (four seconds in, hold four seconds, six seconds out) during an attack to activate the parasympathetic nervous system and reduce hyperventilation.
  \item Use grounding techniques (such as the 5-4-3-2-1 sensory method) to redirect focus away from catastrophic thoughts during acute episodes.
  \item Identify and avoid triggers: limit caffeine, alcohol, and stimulants; ensure regular meals to prevent hypoglycaemia.
  \item Engage in regular aerobic exercise (30 minutes daily, five days per week) to reduce baseline anxiety and panic frequency through endorphin release.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Detailed history of attack frequency, duration, triggers, and associated symptoms; screen for panic disorder, agoraphobia, and comorbid depression using validated instruments (GAD-7, PHQ-9).
  \item Physical examination with vital signs, cardiac auscultation, and thyroid palpation to rule out organic causes such as arrhythmia or thyrotoxicosis.
  \item Laboratory workup: TSH, glucose, electrolytes, and ECG to exclude hyperthyroidism, hypoglycaemia, electrolyte disturbances, or cardiac ischaemia.
  \item Consider 24-hour Holter monitor or event recorder if palpitations or syncope are prominent; refer to cardiology or psychiatry as indicated.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item First-line pharmacotherapy with SSRIs (escitalopram 10--20 mg/day or sertraline 50--200 mg/day) or SNRIs for panic disorder with or without agoraphobia.
  \item Cognitive-behavioural therapy with interoceptive exposure and cognitive restructuring is the most effective psychological treatment; refer to a trained therapist.
  \item For acute breakthrough attacks, short-term use of a benzodiazepine (lorazepam 0.5--1 mg sublingual or alprazolam 0.25--0.5 mg) may be prescribed with strict monitoring for dependence.
  \item Address underlying hormonal factors: hormone therapy for perimenopausal patients may improve panic control by stabilising mood and sleep.
\end{itemize}

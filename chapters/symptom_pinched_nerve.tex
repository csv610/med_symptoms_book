\chapter{Pinched Nerve (Nerve Compression)}

\section*{Definition}
A pinched nerve occurs when excessive pressure is applied to a nerve by surrounding tissues such as bones, muscles, tendons, or discs. It causes pain, tingling, numbness, and weakness in the distribution of the affected nerve.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Compression of a nerve disrupts its blood supply and axonal transport, leading to demyelination and impaired signal conduction. The perineurium and endoneurium swell with edema. If pressure is relieved quickly, recovery is usually complete; prolonged compression can lead to Wallerian degeneration and permanent nerve damage.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Herniated intervertebral disc (cervical or lumbar radiculopathy)
\item Spinal stenosis (narrowing of the spinal canal)
\item Carpal tunnel syndrome (median nerve at the wrist)
\item Cubital tunnel syndrome (ulnar nerve at the elbow)
\item Tarsal tunnel syndrome (tibial nerve at the ankle)
\item Meralgia paresthetica (lateral femoral cutaneous nerve)
\item Piriformis syndrome (sciatic nerve compression)
\item Bone spurs (osteophytes)
\item Arthritis of the spine
\item Repetitive motion injuries
\item Poor posture or prolonged sitting
\item Obesity
\item Pregnancy
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Radiating pain along a nerve pathway
\item Tingling, pins-and-needles sensation (paresthesia)
\item Numbness in a specific distribution
\item Muscle weakness in the affected area
\item Burning sensation
\item Pain worsened by certain positions or movements
\item Muscle atrophy in chronic cases
\item Tinel sign (tingling when tapping over the nerve)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Back pain
\item Neck pain
\item Sciatica
\item Carpal tunnel syndrome
\item Radiculopathy
\item Peripheral neuropathy
\item Muscle weakness
\item Muscle atrophy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care/Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rest the affected area and avoid aggravating activities
\item Apply ice for the first 48 hours, then heat
\item Take OTC anti-inflammatory medications (ibuprofen, naproxen)
\item Practice good posture
\item Use ergonomic equipment (wrist support, standing desk)
\item Stretch gently when symptoms improve
\item Sleep with proper neck and back support
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Detailed neurological examination
\item Nerve conduction studies and electromyography
\item MRI of the affected spine region
\item CT scan with myelography if MRI is contraindicated
\item Ultrasound for peripheral nerve entrapment
\item X-ray to evaluate for bone spurs or arthritis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Conservative: rest, physical therapy, NSAIDs
\item Corticosteroid injections (epidural, nerve sheath)
\item Activity modification and ergonomic changes
\item Surgical decompression for refractory cases (carpal tunnel release, microdiscectomy)
\item Nerve gliding exercises
\item Treatment of underlying cause
\end{itemize}

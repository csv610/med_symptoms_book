\chapter{Warning Signs During Pregnancy}

\section*{Definition}
A set of signs and symptoms during pregnancy that may indicate serious complications requiring urgent medical evaluation, including preeclampsia, placental abruption, preterm labor, or infection.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Pregnancy induces significant physiological changes in the cardiovascular, renal, respiratory, and immune systems. Warning signs reflect pathological decompensation: hypertension and proteinuria indicate preeclampsia (endothelial dysfunction, placental reduced blood flow); vaginal bleeding suggests placental abruption or placenta previa; decreased fetal movement indicates fetal distress; severe abdominal pain may signal placental abruption, uterine rupture, or appendicitis; fever suggests chorioamnionitis or other infection.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Preeclampsia (hypertension, proteinuria, headache, visual changes, RUQ pain)
  \item Placental abruption (vaginal bleeding, abdominal pain, uterine tenderness)
  \item Placenta previa (painless vaginal bleeding in third trimester)
  \item Preterm labor (regular contractions, pelvic pressure, back pain before 37 weeks)
  \item Chorioamnionitis (fever, uterine tenderness, foul amniotic fluid)
  \item Gestational diabetes complications (excessive urination, excessive thirst, blurred vision)
  \item Deep vein thrombosis (unilateral leg swelling, pain, warmth)
  \item Pulmonary embolism (sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, coughing up blood)
  \item Miscarriage (vaginal bleeding, cramping in first trimester)
  \item Ectopic pregnancy (sharp abdominal/pelvic pain, shoulder pain, fainting)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Any vaginal bleeding in pregnancy, severe headache with visual changes, sudden swelling of face/hands, decreased fetal movement, rupture of membranes, or signs of preterm labor before 37 weeks
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Abdominal pain
  \item Vaginal bleeding
  \item Headache
  \item Swelling
  \item Fever
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Any vaginal bleeding, severe headache with visual changes, sudden facial or hand swelling, or decreased fetal movement after 28 weeks warrants immediate medical attention; do not wait for a scheduled appointment.
  \item Do not take aspirin, NSAIDs, or herbal supplements during pregnancy without obstetric clearance, especially if bleeding is present.
  \item Lie on your left side and rest if you experience contractions or pelvic pressure; time the frequency and duration of contractions to report to your provider.
  \item If you experience leakage of fluid, note the colour, odour, and timing; do not use tampons or have intercourse until examined.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Vital signs: blood pressure (two readings four hours apart above 140/90 suggests preeclampsia), pulse, temperature, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation.
  \item Cardiotocography (fetal monitoring) to assess fetal heart rate pattern, variability, accelerations, and decelerations; tocometry to detect uterine contractions.
  \item Laboratory tests: CBC, urinalysis with protein-to-creatinine ratio, liver enzymes (AST, ALT), creatinine, and coagulation profile; Kleihauer-Betke test if Rh-negative with bleeding.
  \item Obstetric ultrasound: fetal presentation, biometry, amniotic fluid index, placental location and grade; cervical length measurement if preterm labour is suspected.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Preeclampsia with severe features: IV antihypertensives (labetalol 20--40 mg IV, hydralazine 5--10 mg IV, or nifedipine 10 mg PO), magnesium sulfate 4 g IV loading for seizure prophylaxis, and delivery planning.
  \item Placental abruption: immediate stabilisation, IV fluids, cross-match for blood transfusion, and emergency caesarean delivery if fetal distress is present.
  \item Preterm labour before 37 weeks: tocolysis, antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone 12 mg IM every 24 hours for two doses) for fetal lung maturity, and group B streptococcus prophylaxis.
  \item Chorioamnionitis: IV antibiotics (ampicillin 2 g IV every six hours plus gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV every 24 hours), expedited delivery, and neonatal evaluation.
\end{itemize}

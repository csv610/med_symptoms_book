\chapter{Amenorrhea (Absence of Menstruation)}

\section*{Definition}
Amenorrhea is the absence of menstrual bleeding in a female of reproductive age. It is classified as primary (failure to begin menstruation by age 15) or secondary (cessation of menstruation for three or more cycles or six months in a woman who previously menstruated).

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Amenorrhea results from disruption of the brain's temperature control center-pituitary-ovarian (HPO) axis. In primary amenorrhea, causes include anatomical abnormalities (imperforate hymen, Mullerian agenesis), gonadal dysgenesis, or brain's temperature control center/pituitary disorders. Secondary amenorrhea typically involves anovulation due to hormonal imbalances, including brain's temperature control center suppression (stress, excessive exercise, low body weight), hyperprolactinemia, thyroid disorders, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), premature ovarian failure, or intrauterine adhesions (Asherman syndrome).

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Pregnancy (most common cause of secondary amenorrhea)
\item Hypothalamic amenorrhea related to stress, excessive exercise, or low body weight
\item Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
\item Premature ovarian failure (primary ovarian insufficiency)
\item Hyperprolactinemia
\item Thyroid disorders (hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism)
\item Asherman syndrome (intrauterine adhesions)
\item Mullerian agenesis (Mayer-Rokitansky-Kuster-Hauser syndrome)
\item Gonadal dysgenesis (Turner syndrome)
\item Medications (contraceptives, antipsychotics, chemotherapy)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item No menstruation by age 15 (primary amenorrhea)
\item Absence of menstruation for three or more cycles in a previously menstruating woman
\item Signs of pregnancy (nausea, breast tenderness, fatigue)
\item Galactorrhea (nipple discharge suggesting hyperprolactinemia)
\item Severe headaches or visual changes (possible pituitary tumor)
\item Signs of androgen excess (hirsutism, acne, male-pattern balding)
\item Hot flashes or vaginal dryness suggesting premature ovarian failure
\item Rapid weight loss or excessive exercise history
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Irregular menstruation (oligomenorrhea)
\item Heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia)
\item Infertility
\item Hirsutism (excessive hair growth)
\item Galactorrhea
\item Hot flashes
\item Vaginal dryness
\item Mood changes
\item Weight gain or weight loss
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care/Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Maintain a healthy body weight through balanced nutrition
\item Reduce excessive exercise if exercise-induced amenorrhea is suspected
\item Manage stress through relaxation techniques and adequate sleep
\item Track menstrual cycles with a calendar or app
\item Discontinue medications that may contribute to amenorrhea under medical guidance
\item Consider dietary adjustments if underweight or malnourished
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Detailed menstrual, sexual, and medication history
\item Pregnancy test (serum hCG)
\item Serum TSH, prolactin, FSH, LH, and estradiol levels
\item Androgen profile (testosterone, DHEA-S) if signs of hyperandrogenism
\item Pelvic ultrasound to evaluate uterine and ovarian anatomy
\item MRI of the brain if pituitary tumor is suspected
\item Hysteroscopy if Asherman syndrome is suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Address underlying cause (weight restoration, stress reduction, medication adjustment)
\item Hormonal therapy with estrogen-progestin replacement for brain's temperature control center amenorrhea
\item Dopamine agonists (cabergoline, bromocriptine) for hyperprolactinemia
\item Thyroid hormone replacement for hypothyroidism
\item Clomiphene citrate or letrozole for ovulation induction in PCOS
\item Surgical correction for anatomical abnormalities or intrauterine adhesions
\item Counseling regarding fertility options if premature ovarian failure is diagnosed
\end{itemize}

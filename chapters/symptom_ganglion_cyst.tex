\chapter{Ganglion Cyst}

\section*{Definition}
A ganglion cyst is a noncancerous, fluid-filled swelling that most commonly develops along the tendons or joints of the wrists, hands, and feet. It is the most common soft-tissue mass of the hand and wrist.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
The exact mechanism is unclear, but ganglion cysts are believed to arise from myxoid degeneration of the connective tissue in joint capsules or tendon sheaths. The cyst fluid resembles synovial fluid but with higher levels of hyaluronic acid. A small stalk connecting to the joint cavity is often present, with a one-way valve mechanism thought to allow fluid accumulation.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Joint or tendon stress from repetitive use
\item Joint or tendon injury
\item Joint or tendon irritation
\item Degenerative changes
\item Connective tissue weakness (generally unknown cause)
\item More common in women aged 20-40 and in gymnasts
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Visible or palpable lump under the skin
\item Most commonly on the dorsum of the wrist
\item Smooth, round, and firm or slightly fluctuant
\item Transilluminates brightly
\item Size may vary over time
\item May or may not be painful
\item Pain or weakness with joint movement
\item Possible tingling or numbness if pressing on a nerve
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Wrist pain
\item Hand or wrist swelling
\item Carpal tunnel syndrome
\item Arthritis of the wrist
\item Tendonitis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Observe and monitor (many resolve spontaneously)
\item Wear a wrist brace or splint to limit movement
\item Avoid activities that aggravate the cyst
\item Avoid attempting to pop or drain the cyst at home
\item Apply over-the-counter pain relievers for discomfort
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clinical examination
\item Transillumination (confirms cystic nature)
\item Ultrasound to confirm cystic nature and rule out solid mass
\item MRI if diagnosis is uncertain or for deep cysts
\item Aspiration reveals thick, clear, jelly-like fluid
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Observation (up to 50 percent resolve spontaneously)
\item Aspiration with a large-bore needle
\item Aspiration followed by corticosteroid injection
\item Surgical excision with ligation of the stalk
\item Splinting to limit movement during recovery
\end{itemize}

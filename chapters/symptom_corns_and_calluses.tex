\chapter{Corns and Calluses}

\section*{Definition}
Corns and calluses are areas of thickened, hardened skin that develop in response to repeated friction or pressure. Calluses are diffuse areas of hyperkeratosis on weight-bearing surfaces, while corns are smaller, well-circumscribed lesions with a central core over bony prominences.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Repeated mechanical trauma stimulates an adaptive thickening of the stratum corneum (hyperkeratosis) as the skin attempts to protect itself. Calluses form on pressure points and are generally painless. Corns form over bony prominences and have a central keratinous core that presses on underlying dermal nerve endings, causing pain.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Ill-fitting footwear (tight, narrow, or high-heeled shoes)
\item Foot deformities (hammertoes, bunions, bone spurs)
\item Abnormal gait or biomechanics
\item Repetitive manual labor (calluses on palms)
\item Walking barefoot on hard surfaces
\item Age-related loss of cushioning fat pads
\item Arthritis causing bony prominences
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Thickened, hardened patches of skin on feet or hands
\item Pain or tenderness when walking or applying pressure
\item Yellowish or grayish discoloration
\item Central hard core (nucleus) characteristic of corns
\item Pain between toes (soft corn)
\item Difficulty finding comfortable shoes
\item Redness or signs of inflammation (possible infection)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Bunions (hallux valgus)
\item Hammer toe deformity
\item Flat feet (pes planus)
\item Foot pain (metatarsalgia)
\item Plantar warts (may be confused with calluses)
\item Athlete's foot (tinea pedis)
\item Skin fissures (cracks in thickened skin)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care/Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Soak feet in warm water and gently file with a pumice stone
\item Apply moisturizing cream containing urea or salicylic acid
\item Use corn pads or donut-shaped felt pads to relieve pressure
\item Wear properly fitted shoes with a wide toe box
\item Avoid cutting corns or calluses with sharp instruments
\item Use OTC salicylic acid patches for corns
\item Wear padded gloves for hand calluses
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clinical examination and inspection of the lesion
\item Distinguish from plantar warts
\item Evaluate footwear for proper fit
\item Assess foot biomechanics and gait
\item X-ray if underlying bone spur suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Pumice stone debridement after soaking
\item Salicylic acid 40 percent patches for corns
\item Paramedical or podiatric debridement
\item Custom orthotics to redistribute pressure
\item Padding and cushioning (felt pads, silicone toe separators)
\item Surgical correction of underlying foot deformity if needed
\item Proper footwear modification
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Acute Pain}

\section*{Definition}
Acute pain is a discrete, self-limited sensory and emotional experience lasting less than 3 months, typically with an identifiable nociceptive, inflammatory, or neuropathic trigger. It serves as a physiologic warning signal of actual or impending tissue damage.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Acute pain arises from activation of peripheral nociceptors by mechanical, thermal, or chemical stimuli, transmitting signals via A-delta and C fibers to the dorsal horn of your spinal cord. Ascending spinothalamic tract projections to the thalamus and somatosensory cortex enable localization, while parallel limbic connections mediate the affective component. Local release of prostaglandins, bradykinin, and substance P sensitizes nociceptors, lowering activation thresholds.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Traumatic:} Lacerations, fractures, burns, contusions, crush injuries
 \item \textbf{Inflammatory:} Infection, arthritis, tendinitis, bursitis, abscess
 \item \textbf{Visceral:} Renal colic, biliary colic, myocardial ischemia, pancreatitis, peritonitis
 \item \textbf{Post-surgical:} Incisional pain, tissue retraction, drain sites
 \item \textbf{Neuropathic:} Acute herpes zoster, nerve entrapment, radiculopathy
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
 \item Pain is severe, progressive, or accompanied by fever, hypotension, or altered mental status
 \item Associated with chest pressure, shortness of breath, or sweating
 \item Abdominal pain with rigidity, guarding, or vomiting
 \item Pain following trauma with deformity, open wound, or inability to bear weight
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Tenderness, swelling, erythema, muscle spasm, guarding
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} Acute pain that persists beyond tissue healing time (3--6 months) may transition to chronic pain, requiring a multidisciplinary management approach.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Rest, ice or heat therapy, and elevation of affected area
 \item Over-the-counter analgesics such as acetaminophen or NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) as tolerated
 \item Wound care for minor injuries: cleansing, dressing, and monitoring for infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Detailed history including onset, location, quality, severity (0--10 scale), duration, and aggravating/alleviating factors
 \item Focused physical examination including inspection, palpation, range of motion, and neurologic assessment
 \item Targeted imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI) or laboratory studies (CBC, CRP, ESR, troponin) based on clinical suspicion
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Pharmacologic: Non-opioid analgesics (acetaminophen, NSAIDs) as first-line; short-course opioids for moderate--severe acute pain when indicated
 \item Non-pharmacologic: Physical therapy, transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS), acupuncture, cognitive behavioral therapy
 \item Interventional: Nerve blocks, joint injections, or epidural analgesia for selected conditions
\end{itemize}

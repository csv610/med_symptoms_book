\chapter{Vision Blurred}

\section*{Definition}
Loss sharpness objects appear out focus transient persistent.

\section*{Pathophysiology}
Refractive error ocular media opacity retinal optic nerve neurological causes all possible ranging benign vision correction needs emergent neurological ophthalmological emergencies requiring prompt differentiation.

\section*{Etiology}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Refractive error myopia hyperopia astigmatism presbyopia
  \item Cataracts
  \item Glaucoma
  \item Diabetic retinopathy
  \item Migraine aura
\end{itemize}

\section*{Indications for Evaluation}
Seek care if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Severe or worsening symptoms
  \item Symptoms lasting more than Evaluate persistent sudden change visual field defects floaters flashes pain associated redness discharge photophobia double vision diplopia
  \item Accompanied by other concerning signs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Eye pain headaches dizziness balance problems speech difficulty facial weakness arm numbness drooping mouth sudden onset suggest stroke TIA emergency evaluation required
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Note:} Always consider serious underlying conditions when evaluating persistent vision blurred.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Rest the eyes using the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
  \item Apply artificial tears or lubricating eye drops for dryness — avoid drops that claim to 'get the red out' which can cause rebound redness.
  \item Use a warm compress for blepharitis or styes; use a cold compress for allergic conjunctivitis or swelling.
  \item Remove contact lenses immediately and switch to glasses if eye irritation develops.
  \item Seek urgent evaluation for eye pain, vision changes, light sensitivity, or chemical exposure.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnostic Approach}
\begin{itemize}
  \item History covers onset, duration, visual changes, pain character, discharge, contact lens use, and trauma.
  \item Visual acuity testing and slit-lamp examination are essential components of the eye evaluation.
  \item Fluorescein staining identifies corneal abrasions or ulcers; tonometry measures intraocular pressure.
  \item Fundoscopic examination evaluates the retina, optic disc, and vasculature for pathology.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment / Management Overview}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Infectious conjunctivitis is treated with appropriate topical antibiotics (bacterial) or antihistamines (allergic).
  \item Dry eye disease management includes artificial tears, punctal plugs, topical cyclosporine, and lifestyle modifications.
  \item Corneal abrasions heal with antibiotic ointment and pressure patching; deep ulcers require ophthalmology referral.
  \item Sudden vision loss, retinal detachment, or acute angle-closure glaucoma are ophthalmologic emergencies requiring immediate specialist care.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Hot Flushes (Hot Flashes)}

\section*{Definition}
Sudden episodes of intense heat sensation spreading over the upper body and face, often accompanied by sweating, palpitations, and anxiety, primarily associated with hormonal changes.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Estrogen withdrawal (menopausal transition) alters hypothalamic thermoregulatory set-point, narrowing the thermoneutral zone so minor core temperature elevations trigger heat-loss responses (widening of blood vessels, sweating). Fluctuations in luteinizing hormone and norepinephrine also contribute to vasomotor instability.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Menopause (natural or surgical)
  \item Perimenopause
  \item Medication side effects (SSRIs, tamoxifen, raloxifene)
  \item Thyroid disorders (hyperthyroidism)
  \item Cancer treatment (chemotherapy, radiation, hormone therapy)
  \item Orchidectomy (in men)
  \item Carcinoid syndrome
  \item Adrenal insufficiency
  \item Alcohol or caffeine triggers
  \item Anxiety or panic disorder
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Hot flushes with fever, night sweats with weight loss, or flushes interfering significantly with quality of life
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Night sweats
  \item Palpitations
  \item Anxiety
  \item Sleep disturbance
  \item Mood swings
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Dress in layers of natural fibres (cotton, linen) so you can remove clothing as a flush begins; keep a handheld fan or cool water spray nearby.
  \item Identify and avoid personal triggers such as hot drinks, spicy foods, alcohol, caffeine, and stressful situations.
  \item Practice slow, deep breathing (paced respiration at 6-8 breaths per minute) at the onset of a hot flush to reduce intensity.
  \item Maintain a cool sleeping environment with breathable bedding and a fan to reduce night sweats and sleep disruption.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Menstrual history and symptom diary (frequency, duration, triggers of flushes) to establish the pattern and severity of vasomotor symptoms.
  \item Hormonal profile including serum FSH, oestradiol, and thyroid function tests to confirm menopausal status and exclude thyroid disorders.
  \item Prolactin level is checked if galactorrhoea is present; 24-hour urinary 5-HIAA is ordered if carcinoid syndrome is suspected.
  \item Referral to a gynaecologist or endocrinologist is arranged for atypical presentations, early-onset menopause, or complex hormonal management.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT) with oestrogen (with or without progestogen) is the most effective treatment for moderate-to-severe vasomotor symptoms; the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration is used.
  \item Non-hormonal pharmacological options include SSRIs/SNRIs (paroxetine, venlafaxine), gabapentin, or clonidine for women who cannot or choose not to take hormones.
  \item Lifestyle modifications — paced respiration, avoiding triggers, keeping cool — remain the foundation of self-management alongside pharmacotherapy.
  \item Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) has evidence for reducing the perceived bother of hot flushes and improving sleep quality.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

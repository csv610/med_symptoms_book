\chapter{Allodynia}

\section*{Definition}
Allodynia is pain due to a stimulus that does not normally provoke pain, classified as mechanical (dynamic or static), thermal (cold or heat), or tactile. It is a hallmark of neuropathic pain and central sensitization, distinct from hyperalgesia (increased pain from a normally painful stimulus).

\section*{Pathophysiology}
Allodynia results from maladaptive plasticity in nociceptive pathways. Peripheral sensitization involves upregulation of sodium channels (Na$_v$1.7, Na$_v$1.8) and TRPV1 receptors on nociceptors, lowering their activation threshold. Central sensitization in the spinal cord involves NMDA receptor activation, loss of GABAergic and glycinergic inhibition, and microglial release of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF). A-beta fibers (normally mechanoreceptive) undergo phenotypic switch and begin expressing substance P and CGRP, allowing low-threshold stimuli to activate second-order nociceptive neurons.

\section*{Etiology}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Neuropathic:} Diabetic peripheral neuropathy, postherpetic neuralgia, trigeminal neuralgia, Fabry disease
  \item \textbf{Central sensitization syndromes:} Fibromyalgia, complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS), migraine
  \item \textbf{Spinal cord injury:} Central pain syndrome, syringomyelia
  \item \textbf{Post-surgical:} Post-mastectomy pain, post-thoracotomy pain, phantom limb pain
  \item \textbf{Infectious:} HIV-associated neuropathy, postherpetic neuralgia induced by varicella-zoster virus
  \item \textbf{Demyelinating:} Multiple sclerosis, Guillain-Barr\'{e} syndrome
  \item \textbf{Chemotherapy-induced:} Platinum-based drugs, taxanes, bortezomib, thalidomide
  \item \textbf{Toxins:} Alcohol-related neuropathy, heavy metal toxicity
\end{itemize}

\section*{Indications for Evaluation}
Seek care if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pain provoked by light touch, clothing, or temperature changes
  \item Pain spreading beyond the initial site of injury or dermatomal distribution
  \item Associated with skin color changes, edema, or sweating (suggesting CRPS)
  \item Progressive or disabling pain not responsive to over-the-counter analgesics
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pain with light brushing, clothing contact, or wind (mechanical allodynia)
  \item Pain with cold or warm stimuli not typically painful (thermal allodynia)
  \item Hyperalgesia (increased pain to normally painful stimuli)
  \item Paresthesias, dysesthesias, or burning sensation
  \item Autonomic changes: skin color, temperature, sweating, edema
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Note:} Allodynia is a cardinal feature of central sensitization; the presence of mechanical allodynia predicts response to gabapentinoids and tricyclic antidepressants.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Protective clothing to minimize skin irritation from light touch
  \item Avoidance of known triggers (e.g., tight clothing, temperature extremes)
  \item Desensitization therapy with graded exposure to textures and temperatures
  \item Over-the-counter topical agents: lidocaine patches, capsaicin cream (low concentration)
  \item Regular sleep hygiene and stress reduction to lower central excitability
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnostic Approach}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Quantitative sensory testing (QST) for detection thresholds of light touch, brush, pinprick, and temperature
  \item Neurological examination including assessment of sensation, reflexes, and motor function
  \item Skin biopsy for intraepidermal nerve fiber density (small fiber neuropathy)
  \item Nerve conduction studies and electromyography for large fiber involvement
  \item MRI of the spine or brain if central causes are suspected
  \item Laboratory evaluation: fasting glucose, HbA1c, vitamin B12, TSH, ANA, ESR, HIV, hepatitis panel
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment / Management Overview}
\begin{itemize}
  \item First-line pharmacotherapy: Gabapentinoids (gabapentin, pregabalin), tricyclic antidepressants (amitriptyline, nortriptyline), SNRIs (duloxetine, venlafaxine)
  \item Topical agents: Lidocaine 5\% patch, capsaicin 8\% patch, amitriptyline-ketamine compounded cream
  \item Second-line: Tramadol, tapentadol, carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine (for trigeminal neuralgia)
  \item Interventional: Sympathetic nerve blocks, spinal cord stimulation, dorsal root ganglion stimulation
  \item Physical and occupational therapy with graded motor imagery and desensitization
  \item Cognitive-behavioral therapy and pain psychology for central sensitization syndrome
\end{itemize}

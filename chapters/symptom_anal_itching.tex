\chapter{Anal Itching (Pruritus Ani)}

\section*{Definition}
Anal itching (itching ani) is an intense, chronic itching of the skin around the anus. It affects up to 5 percent of the population and is more common in men. The condition can significantly impair quality of life due to discomfort, sleep disturbance, and social embarrassment.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Pruritus ani involves irritation of the perianal skin, which triggers the itch-scratch cycle. Scratching causes further skin damage and inflammation, perpetuating the itch. The perianal area has a thin, moist stratum corneum that is vulnerable to irritants. Fecal moisture, enzymes, and bile salts can damage your skin barrier. Secondary infection with Candida albicans or bacteria can exacerbate inflammation.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Poor hygiene or excessive cleaning (soap residue, vigorous wiping)
\item Fecal soiling/incontinence leading to perianal moisture
\item Hemorrhoids or anal fissures
\item Pinworm infection (Enterobius vermicularis), especially in children
\item Candida (yeast) infection, particularly in diabetics
\item Contact dermatitis from soaps, lotions, toilet paper, or laundry detergents
\item Psoriasis or seborrheic dermatitis involving the perianal area
\item Food irritants (caffeine, spicy foods, citrus, chocolate, beer)
\item Antibiotic use leading to secondary yeast overgrowth
\item Anal fistula or other anorectal conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Persistent perianal itching lasting more than one to two weeks
\item Itching accompanied by rectal bleeding or discharge
\item Visible worms in stool (suggesting pinworm infection)
\item Perianal pain, swelling, or tenderness
\item Skin changes such as redness, fissures, or lichenification
\item Nocturnal itching (common with pinworms)
\item History of diabetes, immunosuppression, or inflammatory bowel disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Hemorrhoids
\item Anal fissure
\item Rectal discharge
\item Rectal pain
\item Perianal rash
\item Diarrhea or fecal incontinence
\item Vaginal itching (itching vulvae)
\item Pinworm infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clean the perianal area gently with plain warm water after bowel movements
\item Pat dry (do not rub) and avoid harsh soaps or fragranced wipes
\item Apply a thin layer of petroleum jelly or zinc oxide paste as a barrier
\item Wear loose-fitting, breathable cotton underwear
\item Avoid scratching; keep fingernails short
\item Eliminate potential dietary triggers (caffeine, spicy foods, citrus)
\item Use over-the-counter hydrocortisone cream (1%) sparingly for short-term relief
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Visual inspection of the perianal area
\item Cellophane tape test for pinworm eggs (collected in the morning)
\item Stool examination for ova and parasites if pinworm is suspected
\item Skin scraping for fungal culture if Candida is suspected
\item Anoscopy or sigmoidoscopy if underlying anorectal pathology is suspected
\item Patch testing if contact dermatitis is suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Treat underlying cause (pinworm eradication, antifungal for Candida)
\item Short course of topical corticosteroids for inflammation
\item Topical antihistamines or pramoxine for symptomatic relief
\item Behavioral modification to break the itch-scratch cycle
\item Sitz baths with plain warm water after bowel movements
\item Dietary modification to eliminate identified triggers
\item Referral to a dermatologist or colorectal specialist for refractory cases
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Pelvic Organ Prolapse}

\section*{Definition}
Descent of one or more pelvic organs (uterus, bladder, rectum, bowel, or vaginal vault) into or beyond the vaginal canal due to weakened pelvic floor support structures.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Pelvic organ prolapse results from compromise of the pelvic floor musculature (levator ani complex) and connective tissue (endopelvic fascia, uterosacral ligaments). Oestrogen maintains collagen and elastin in pelvic support tissues; declining oestrogen after menopause reduces tissue strength and elasticity. Vaginal childbirth (particularly multiple or traumatic deliveries) stretches and damages the levator ani and pudendal nerve. Increased intra-abdominal pressure (obesity, chronic cough, constipation, heavy lifting) accelerates prolapse progression. Prolapse is graded I-IV based on descent relative to the hymen.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Menopause (oestrogen deficiency weakening connective tissue)
  \item Vaginal childbirth (especially multiple, instrumental, or macrosomic deliveries)
  \item Advanced age
  \item Obesity (increased intra-abdominal pressure)
  \item Chronic constipation or straining
  \item Chronic cough (smoking, COPD, asthma)
  \item Heavy lifting (occupational or recreational)
  \item Connective tissue disorders (Ehlers-Danlos, Marfan)
  \item Prior pelvic surgery (hysterectomy)
  \item Genetic predisposition (family history of prolapse)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Sensation of a bulge or pressure in the vagina, pelvic heaviness, urinary or faecal incontinence, or difficulty emptying bladder/bowel
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pelvic pain
  \item Urinary incontinence
  \item Urinary frequency
  \item Back pain
  \item Painful sex
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Perform Kegel exercises (pelvic floor muscle contractions, three sets of 8--12 repetitions daily) to strengthen the levator ani and improve symptomatic support.
  \item Avoid heavy lifting and chronic straining with bowel movements (use stool softeners and a high-fibre diet); manage chronic cough to reduce intra-abdominal pressure.
  \item Consider a vaginal pessary fitted by a healthcare provider as a non-surgical option for symptom relief; follow up every three to six months for cleaning and assessment.
  \item Achieve and maintain a healthy body weight; weight loss of 5--10\% significantly reduces prolapse symptoms and progression.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pelvic examination using the POP-Q system to grade prolapse of each compartment (anterior, apical, posterior) at rest and with Valsalva manoeuvre.
  \item Assessment of pelvic floor muscle strength (Oxford grading scale) and coordination; evaluate for levator ani avulsion if there is a history of traumatic delivery.
  \item Urodynamic studies if urinary incontinence or voiding dysfunction is present; post-void residual volume to assess for urinary retention.
  \item Referral to urogynaecology or pelvic floor physiotherapy for comprehensive management; consider MRI defecography if posterior compartment or defecatory dysfunction is suspected.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pelvic floor muscle training (supervised physiotherapy, biofeedback, or electrical stimulation) as first-line treatment for mild-to-moderate prolapse (stages I--II).
  \item Vaginal oestrogen therapy (topical cream, tablet, or ring) to improve tissue quality and elasticity, particularly in postmenopausal women and as an adjunct to pessary use.
  \item Pessary fitting and follow-up every three to six months for cleaning and assessment of vaginal mucosa integrity.
  \item Surgical repair (anterior or posterior colporrhaphy, sacrospinous ligament fixation, or sacrocolpopexy) for severe or symptomatic prolapse (stage III--IV) or when conservative measures fail.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Urethral Discharge}

\section*{Definition}
Urethral discharge is any fluid exuding from the urethral meatus that is abnormal in character, quantity, or timing, distinct from normal secretions or urine. It is the cardinal symptom of male urethritis and warrants prompt evaluation for sexually transmitted infections.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Infection of the urethral epithelium by pathogens triggers an inflammatory response with neutrophil recruitment and exudate formation. The discharge characteristics reflect the underlying pathogen: Gram-negative intracellular diplococci (Neisseria gonorrhoeae) produce a purulent, copious discharge, while Chlamydia trachomatis typically induces a milder, mucoid or watery discharge. Chronic inflammation can lead to urethral stricture formation from scarring and scarring.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Infectious urethritis: Neisseria gonorrhoeae (gonococcal urethritis), Chlamydia trachomatis (non-gonococcal urethritis), Mycoplasma genitalium, Ureaplasma urealyticum, Trichomonas vaginalis, Herpes simplex virus (primary infection), Adenovirus, Candida albicans (usually in men with diabetes or after sexual contact with infected partner)
  \item Non-infectious causes: Reactive arthritis (Reiter syndrome -- urethritis, conjunctivitis, arthritis), Urethral stricture, Foreign body, Urethral trauma (catheterization, instrumentation), Chemical irritants (soaps, spermicides), Lichen sclerosus
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Any urethral discharge is observed, regardless of quantity or color
  \item Discharge accompanied by painful urination, penile pain, or genital ulceration
  \item Recent unprotected sexual contact or multiple partners
  \item Fever, testicular pain, or inguinal lymphadenopathy develops
  \item Recurrent discharge after prior treatment (possible reinfection or antimicrobial resistance)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item painful urination
  \item Urinary frequency
  \item Urethral itching or tingling
  \item Meatal redness
  \item Penile swelling
  \item Testicular pain
  \item Inguinal lymphadenopathy
  \item Genital ulcers
  \item Joint pain (reactive arthritis)
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} N. gonorrhoeae and C. trachomatis co-infection is common; people treated for gonorrhea should also receive empiric therapy for chlamydia.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Abstain from sexual activity until treatment is completed and symptoms resolve.
  \item Avoid applying any creams, ointments, or home remedies to the urethra.
  \item Wash the genital area gently with warm water; avoid harsh soaps or antiseptics.
  \item Inform recent sexual partners so they can be evaluated and treated.
  \item Complete the full course of prescribed antibiotics even if symptoms improve.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Gram stain of urethral discharge: $>$2 WBC per oil-immersion field and intracellular Gram-negative diplococci confirm gonorrhea.
  \item Nucleic acid amplification testing (NAAT) of urine or urethral swab for N. gonorrhoeae and C. trachomatis.
  \item NAAT or culture for M. genitalium if symptoms persist after gonorrhea/chlamydia treatment.
  \item HIV and syphilis serology offered to all people with urethral discharge.
  \item Urinalysis and urine culture to rule out concurrent UTI or prostatitis.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Gonococcal urethritis: Single-dose ceftriaxone 500 mg IM plus azithromycin 1 g orally (or doxycycline if azithromycin not recommended).
  \item Non-gonococcal urethritis: Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 7 days; azithromycin 1 g single dose as alternative.
  \item M. genitalium: Azithromycin (extended regimen) or moxifloxacin for resistant cases.
  \item HSV urethritis: Acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir for primary or recurrent episodes.
  \item Partner notification, treatment, and test-of-cure as recommended by local public health guidelines.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Back Pain}

\section*{Definition}
Back pain is discomfort anywhere along the spine, from the neck to the lower back. It may arise from muscles, joints, discs, nerves, bones, or internal organs.

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
 \item Seek urgent care for new bladder or bowel dysfunction, saddle numbness, progressive weakness, fever, major trauma, or a history of cancer or immune suppression.
 \item Arrange medical assessment for persistent, recurrent, unexplained, or night-waking pain.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How It Develops}
Originates from muscles, nerves, bones or joints; classified acute/subacute/chronic with mechanical vs inflammatory patterns.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Muscle strain
 \item Herniated disc
 \item Degenerative disc disease
 \item Osteoarthritis
 \item Osteoporotic fracture
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Stiffness
 \item Limited mobility
 \item Radiating leg pain
 \item Muscle spasms
 \item Reduced flexibility
\end{itemize}

\section*{Medical Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
 \item History and examination assess neurologic function, gait, range of motion, systemic symptoms, trauma, and red flags.
 \item Imaging is generally reserved for red flags, major trauma, neurologic deficit, or persistent symptoms when results would change management.
 \item Blood tests are considered when infection, inflammatory disease, malignancy, or another systemic cause is suspected.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Most nonspecific back pain is managed with education, exercise or physical therapy, and selected analgesics.
 \item Radiculopathy, fracture, infection, inflammatory disease, or malignancy requires cause-specific treatment and sometimes specialist referral.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care and Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Stay gently active and avoid prolonged bed rest; temporarily modify activities that clearly worsen pain.
 \item Heat or ice may provide short-term relief, and acetaminophen or an NSAID may be appropriate if safe for you and used as labelled.
 \item Seek urgent care for new bladder or bowel dysfunction, saddle numbness, progressive leg weakness, fever, major trauma, or a history of cancer or immune suppression.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Abdominal Mass}

\section*{Definition}
An abdominal mass is any abnormal enlargement or palpable swelling within the abdominal cavity, detected on physical examination or imaging. The clinical significance depends on location, consistency, mobility, tenderness, and your age; malignancy must be considered in the appropriate context.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Abdominal masses arise from organomegaly (liver, spleen, kidney), neoplastic growth (primary or metastatic), fluid collections (abscess, cyst), or anatomic abnormalities (aneurysm, hernia). The mass effect may compress adjacent structures, impair organ function, or cause obstructive symptoms.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Right upper quadrant:} Hepatomegaly (cirrhosis, hepatitis, malignancy), gallbladder hydrops or empyema, hepatic adenoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, colonic mass
 \item \textbf{Epigastric:} Pancreatic pseudocyst, pancreatic mass, gastric tumor, abdominal aortic aneurysm (pulsatile, greater than 3 cm), left lobe hepatomegaly
 \item \textbf{Left upper quadrant:} Splenomegaly (portal hypertension, lymphoma, infection, hemolytic anemia), polycystic kidney, gastric or colonic mass
 \item \textbf{Flank/Lumbar:} Renal mass (hypernephroma, hydronephrosis, polycystic kidney), adrenal mass (pheochromocytoma, adenoma), psoas abscess
 \item \textbf{Pelvic:} Ovarian mass (cyst, neoplasm), uterine fibroids, pelvic kidney, bladder distension, diverticular abscess
 \item \textbf{Diffuse/generalized:} Carcinomatosis, tuberculosis, lymphoma, intra-abdominal abscess, massive ascites
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
 \item Newly discovered abdominal mass, especially in you over 50 or with weight loss
 \item Rapidly growing mass or increasing abdominal girth
 \item Associated pain, obstructive symptoms (vomiting, constipation), or GI bleeding
 \item Pulsatile mass with back pain (suspected rupturing AAA)
 \item Fever, night sweats, or jaundice accompanying the mass
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Abdominal pain, distension, early satiety, nausea, vomiting, constipation, weight loss, palpable lump, jaundice
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} A pulsatile, expansile abdominal mass in you over 60 with back pain is an abdominal aortic aneurysm until proven otherwise and requires urgent vascular evaluation.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Avoid aggressive palpation or manipulation of a known abdominal mass
 \item Keep a symptom diary noting changes in size, pain, bowel habits, and weight
 \item Maintain a bland diet to minimize GI symptoms from mass effect
 \item Avoid heavy lifting or straining (particularly if AAA is suspected)
 \item Seek medical evaluation for any new or changing abdominal mass rather than observing at home
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Comprehensive history and physical: location, size, consistency, mobility, tenderness, pulsatility, percussion
 \item Abdominal ultrasound: first-line imaging for origin (solid vs. cystic), size, and organ of origin
 \item CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast for characterization, staging, and guiding biopsy
 \item MRI for problem-solving: liver lesions, pancreatic masses, pelvic pathology
 \item Laboratory: CBC, LFTs, renal function, LDH, tumor markers (CEA, CA19-9, AFP, CA-125) based on suspected origin
 \item Image-guided biopsy (CT or ultrasound) for tissue diagnosis when malignancy is suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Treatment is directed by cause: surgical resection for resectable malignancies, chemotherapy for metastatic disease
 \item Observation with serial imaging for benign, asymptomatic masses (hepatic hemangioma, simple renal cyst)
 \item Drainage of abscesses (percutaneous or surgical); antibiotics for infected collections
 \item Endovascular or open repair for AAA greater than 5.5 cm or symptomatic
 \item Palliative measures (debulking, stenting, pain control) for advanced or unresectable disease
\end{itemize}

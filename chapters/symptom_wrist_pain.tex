\chapter{Wrist Pain}

\section*{Definition}
Pain or discomfort localized to the wrist joint and surrounding structures, commonly resulting from overuse, trauma, or inflammatory conditions.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
The wrist's complex anatomy of eight carpal bones, multiple ligaments, and flexor/extensor tendons makes it susceptible to injury. Pain localizes anatomically: radial side (De Quervain tenosynovitis, scaphoid fracture, thumb arthritis), ulnar side (TFCC tear, extensor carpi ulnaris tendinopathy), dorsal (ganglion cyst, intersection syndrome), volar (carpal tunnel syndrome).

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item De Quervain tenosynovitis
  \item Carpal tunnel syndrome
  \item Wrist sprain or fracture (scaphoid, distal radius)
  \item Osteoarthritis or post-traumatic arthritis
  \item Ganglion cyst
  \item Rheumatoid arthritis
  \item Gout or pseudogout
  \item Ligamentous injury (TFCC tear)
  \item Tendinopathy (extensor carpi radialis, flexor carpi ulnaris)
  \item Kienböck disease (avascular tissue death of lunate)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Wrist pain after injury, inability to move the wrist, severe swelling, numbness in fingers, or fever
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Hand numbness
  \item Finger pain
  \item Swelling
  \item Weak grip
  \item Snapping sensation
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Rest the wrist in a neutral position; use a prefabricated wrist splint (cock-up brace) for carpal tunnel syndrome or tendinopathy, particularly at night to prevent flexed sleeping positions.
  \item Apply ice (15 minutes every two to three hours) for acute pain and swelling; use heat for chronic stiffness after the initial 48-hour period.
  \item Modify repetitive hand activities: use ergonomic tools (split keyboard, cushioned mouse pad), take frequent breaks, and alternate hands for tasks.
  \item Take NSAIDs (ibuprofen 400--600 mg three times daily with food) for five to seven days for inflammatory or overuse wrist pain.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Anatomical localisation of tenderness: anatomical snuffbox (scaphoid fracture), radial styloid (De Quervain), volar wrist (carpal tunnel), ulnar wrist (TFCC tear), dorsal wrist (ganglion).
  \item Neurovascular examination: Tinel sign and Phalen manoeuvre for carpal tunnel syndrome; Allen test for radial and ulnar artery patency; capillary refill and sensation testing.
  \item Plain radiographs (PA, lateral, oblique views) for suspected fracture, arthritis, or dislocation; scaphoid view if snuffbox tenderness with negative initial X-rays.
  \item Ultrasound or MRI for soft tissue pathology: tenosynovitis, TFCC tear, ganglion cyst, ligament injury, or Kienböck disease.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item De Quervain tenosynovitis: thumb spica splint, NSAIDs, corticosteroid injection into the first dorsal compartment; surgical release for refractory cases.
  \item Carpal tunnel syndrome: night splint (wrist in neutral position), NSAIDs, corticosteroid injection into the carpal tunnel; open or endoscopic release if EMG shows moderate-to-severe compression.
  \item Scaphoid fracture: thumb spica cast for 6--12 weeks; internal fixation with a Herbert screw for displaced fractures or non-union.
  \item Wrist arthritis: NSAIDs, activity modification, intra-articular corticosteroid injection; arthroscopic debridement, partial wrist fusion, or total wrist arthroplasty for end-stage disease.
\end{itemize}

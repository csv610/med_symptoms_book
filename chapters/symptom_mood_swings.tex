\chapter{Mood Swings}

\section*{Definition}
Rapid or extreme fluctuations in emotional state, shifting between euphoria, irritability, sadness, or anger over short periods without clear precipitants.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Mood regulation involves complex interplay between neurotransmitters (serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine), hormones (estrogen, progesterone, cortisol), and neural circuits (prefrontal cortex, amygdala, hippocampus). Disruption from hormonal changes, stress, sleep deprivation, or psychiatric conditions produces emotional lability.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) or PMDD
  \item Perimenopause and menopause
  \item Pregnancy and postpartum period
  \item Bipolar disorder
  \item Depression or anxiety disorders
  \item Chronic stress
  \item Sleep deprivation
  \item Substance use or withdrawal
  \item Thyroid disorders (hyperthyroidism)
  \item Medication side effects (corticosteroids, antidepressants)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Mood swings with thoughts of self-harm, significant functional impairment, or accompanying severe depression or mania
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Irritability
  \item Fatigue
  \item Sleep changes
  \item Anxiety
  \item Appetite changes
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Track mood patterns using a diary or app (recording mood, energy, sleep, and menstrual cycle phase) to identify triggers and cyclical patterns.
  \item Practise the STOP technique when you feel a mood shift: Stop, Take a breath, Observe what you are feeling, and Proceed with intention rather than reacting.
  \item Maintain a consistent sleep-wake schedule, as sleep deprivation is one of the strongest precipitants of emotional lability.
  \item Engage in regular aerobic exercise (30 minutes daily) which stabilises mood through endorphin release and serotonin regulation.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Detailed history including onset, duration, frequency, triggers, and screening for underlying bipolar disorder, depression, anxiety, and PMS/PMDD.
  \item Mood diary reviewed over at least 2 menstrual cycles to identify cyclical patterns linked to the menstrual cycle.
  \item Thyroid function tests and hormonal profile (FSH, oestradiol, progesterone) are checked if perimenopause or thyroid dysfunction is suspected.
  \item Referral to a psychiatrist is indicated for suspected bipolar disorder, severe mood swings with functional impairment, or if there are thoughts of self-harm.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Menopausal hormone therapy with oestrogen smooths the hormonal fluctuations that trigger mood instability in perimenopausal women.
  \item SSRIs (such as sertraline, fluoxetine, or citalopram) are effective for mood swings associated with PMS/PMDD, depression, and anxiety disorders.
  \item Mood stabilisers (lamotrigine, valproate) or atypical antipsychotics are prescribed by a psychiatrist for bipolar spectrum mood swings.
  \item Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) and dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) teach emotion regulation skills and reduce the intensity of mood fluctuations.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

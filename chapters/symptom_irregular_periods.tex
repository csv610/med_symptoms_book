\chapter{Irregular Periods}

\section*{Definition}
Menstrual cycles that vary significantly from the typical 21-35 day pattern, including absent periods (amenorrhea), infrequent periods (oligomenorrhea), or unpredictable cycle length.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Normal menstruation requires intact hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis with appropriate hormone levels. Disruption at any level causes irregular bleeding: hypothalamus (stress, exercise, weight loss), pituitary (prolactinoma), ovarian (PCOS, perimenopause, premature ovarian failure), uterine (fibroids, polyps), or endocrine (thyroid, adrenal) causes.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
  \item Perimenopause (transition to menopause)
  \item Thyroid disorders (hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism)
  \item Hyperprolactinemia
  \item Stress or significant weight changes
  \item Excessive exercise (athletic amenorrhea)
  \item Eating disorders (anorexia nervosa)
  \item Uterine fibroids or polyps
  \item Primary ovarian insufficiency (premature menopause)
  \item Hormonal contraception effects
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Missed periods for 3+ cycles, very heavy bleeding, bleeding between periods, or difficulty conceiving
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Heavy bleeding
  \item Pelvic pain
  \item Weight changes
  \item Hair growth changes
  \item Acne
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Keep a menstrual calendar or use a period-tracking app to record cycle dates, duration, flow heaviness, and associated symptoms for at least 3 months.
  \item Maintain a stable body weight through balanced nutrition and regular moderate exercise, as both overweight and underweight can disrupt menstrual cycles.
  \item Manage stress through mindfulness, yoga, or counselling since psychological stress affects the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis.
  \item Avoid smoking and limit alcohol intake, as both are associated with menstrual irregularities and early menopause.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Menstrual history (age at menarche, cycle pattern, duration, flow, pain) and gynaecological examination including speculum and bimanual exam.
  \item Hormonal panel: serum FSH, LH, oestradiol, progesterone, prolactin, and thyroid function tests to assess the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis.
  \item Transvaginal ultrasound to evaluate ovarian morphology (PCOS), endometrial thickness, fibroids, and polyps.
  \item Referral to a gynaecologist or endocrinologist is indicated for persistent irregularity, infertility concerns, or suspected pituitary pathology.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Hormonal contraception (combined oral contraceptive pill, patch, or vaginal ring) is first-line treatment to regulate cycles in women who are not trying to conceive.
  \item For PCOS-related irregularity, weight loss (5-10\% body weight reduction improves ovulation), metformin, or letrozole/clomiphene may be used depending on fertility goals.
  \item Thyroid disorders or hyperprolactinaemia are treated with appropriate hormone replacement (levothyroxine) or dopamine agonists (cabergoline) respectively.
  \item Progestin therapy (cyclic or continuous) or a levonorgestrel intrauterine system can regulate bleeding and provide endometrial protection in perimenopausal women.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Binge Eating}

\section*{Definition}
Binge eating is the consumption of an objectively large amount of food within a discrete period (e.g., 2 hours) accompanied by a sense of loss of control. For binge-eating disorder (BED), episodes occur at least once weekly for 3 months and are associated with marked distress.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Binge eating involves dysregulation of homeostatic (hypothalamus) and hedonic (mesolimbic dopamine) feeding circuits. Altered signaling in the nucleus accumbens, orbitofrontal cortex, and insula impairs satiety detection and reward processing, while heightened reactivity to food cues and emotional triggers drives compulsive overconsumption.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Psychiatric:} Binge-eating disorder, bulimia nervosa, atypical anorexia nervosa
 \item \textbf{Psychological factors:} Negative affect, emotional dysregulation, impulsivity, low self-esteem, body dissatisfaction, dietary restraint
 \item \textbf{Genetic:} Strong heritability (40--60\%); variants in BDNF, FTO, and MC4R genes
 \item \textbf{Environmental:} Childhood obesity, history of weight-based teasing, trauma, food insecurity
 \item \textbf{Medical:} Prader-Willi syndrome, Kleine-Levin syndrome, hypothalamic lesions
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
 \item Binge episodes occur at least weekly for 3 months or more
 \item Eating is accompanied by extreme distress, shame, or impaired functioning
 \item There is purging behavior, significant weight changes, or medical complications (e.g., metabolic syndrome)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Obesity, emotional eating, food cravings, body dissatisfaction, depression, anxiety, substance use disorders
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} Binge-eating disorder is the most common eating disorder and frequently co-occurs with obesity, depression, and metabolic syndrome.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Keep a food-mood diary to identify triggers and patterns
 \item Practice mindful eating: eat slowly, without distractions, and pause between bites
 \item Establish regular meal patterns (3 meals + 1--2 planned snacks) to reduce deprivation
 \item Avoid rigid food rules and dieting that promote restriction-binge cycles
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Clinical interview assessing frequency of binge episodes, loss of control, distress, and compensatory behaviors
 \item Screen for comorbid psychiatric conditions: depression, anxiety, substance use
 \item Physical exam: weight, BMI, waist circumference; signs of metabolic syndrome
 \item Laboratory: fasting glucose, lipid panel, liver enzymes
 \item Validated tools: Eating Disorder Examination (EDE), Binge Eating Scale (BES)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item First-line: Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT-E) or interpersonal psychotherapy for BED
 \item Pharmacotherapy: lisdexamfetamine (FDA-approved for BED), SSRIs (fluoxetine for bulimia nervosa), or topiramate
 \item Nutritional counseling to normalize eating patterns and address weight concerns
 \item Treat comorbid obesity with lifestyle modification and, if indicated, anti-obesity medications or bariatric surgery
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Eye Strain (Asthenopia)}

\section*{Definition}
Ocular discomfort, fatigue, or pain associated with prolonged visual tasks, eye muscle imbalance, or uncorrected refractive errors.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Prolonged near-work (digital screens, reading) causes sustained contraction of ciliary muscle (accommodation) and medial rectus muscles (convergence), leading to muscle fatigue, reduced blink rate, and ocular surface drying. Accommodative spasm may follow prolonged near-focus, causing transient distance blur.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Prolonged screen time (computer vision syndrome / digital eye strain)
  \item Uncorrected refractive errors (myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism, presbyopia)
  \item Poor lighting or glare
  \item Decreased blink rate while concentrating
  \item Dry eye disease
  \item Accommodative or convergence insufficiency
  \item Small font sizes or poor screen contrast
  \item Postural issues (looking up vs. down at screens)
  \item Reading in dim light
  \item Eye muscle imbalance
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Persistent eye strain despite taking breaks and correcting ergonomics, or eye strain with headaches, double vision, or vision changes
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Headache
  \item Blurred vision
  \item Dry eyes
  \item Neck pain
  \item Photophobia
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
  \item Adjust screen brightness, contrast, and font size for comfortable viewing.
  \item Ensure proper lighting — reduce glare and avoid working in dim or overly bright environments.
  \item Blink frequently and use lubricating eye drops to prevent ocular surface drying.
  \item Position your screen at arm's length and slightly below eye level.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Your doctor will ask about your work habits, screen time, lighting conditions, and vision correction.
  \item Visual acuity testing and refraction determine if uncorrected refractive errors are contributing.
  \item Accommodative and convergence testing assess near-vision function.
  \item A slit-lamp examination checks for dry eye disease contributing to symptoms.
  \item Referral to an optometrist or ophthalmologist for a comprehensive eye examination.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Corrective lenses (glasses or contact lenses) for uncorrected refractive errors.
  \item Computer glasses with anti-reflective coating and blue light filtering may reduce strain.
  \item Artificial tears for associated dry eye symptoms.
  \item Ergonomic workstation adjustments and proper screen positioning.
  \item Vision therapy for accommodative or convergence insufficiency.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Stretch Marks (Striae Distensae)}

\section*{Definition}
Stretch marks (striae) are linear scars that develop in the dermis due to rapid stretching of the skin and hormonal factors. They are common during pregnancy, puberty, rapid weight gain, and in conditions such as Cushing syndrome.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Stretch marks result from a combination of mechanical stretching and hormonal changes that disrupt dermal collagen and elastin fibers. Glucocorticoids inhibit fibroblast function, reducing collagen synthesis. The dermis thins and tears, allowing the underlying subcutaneous tissue to show through. Initially, they appear red or purple (striae rubra), then fade to white or silver (striae alba).

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Pregnancy (striae gravidarum)
\item Puberty (rapid growth)
\item Rapid weight gain or obesity
\item Corticosteroid use (topical or systemic)
\item Cushing syndrome
\item Marfan syndrome (connective tissue disorder)
\item Ehlers-Danlos syndrome
\item Bodybuilding (rapid muscle growth)
\item Genetics: family history increases risk
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Linear, parallel streaks on the skin
\item Initially red, purple, or pink (striae rubra)
\item Later become white, silvery, or hypopigmented (striae alba)
\item Most common on abdomen, breasts, thighs, hips, buttocks, and arms
\item Slightly depressed or wrinkled appearance
\item May be itchy when newly formed
\item No pain or other symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Cushing syndrome signs (moon face, buffalo hump, central obesity)
\item Pregnancy-related skin changes
\item Weight gain
\item Obesity
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Keep skin well-moisturized (cocoa butter, shea butter, vitamin E oil)
\item Maintain a stable, healthy weight
\item Stay hydrated
\item Use sun protection on affected areas
\item Apply silicone gel or sheeting for newer stretch marks
\item Avoid prolonged corticosteroid use
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clinical examination is diagnostic
\item Rarely, biopsy for atypical cases
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Topical retinoids (tretinoin) for early striae rubra
\item Pulsed dye laser for red stretch marks
\item Fractional laser resurfacing
\item Microneedling
\item Microdermabrasion
\item Chemical peels
\item No treatment is medically necessary
\end{itemize}

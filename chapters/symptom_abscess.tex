\chapter{Abscess (Skin Abscess)}

\section*{Definition}
A skin abscess is a localized collection of pus within the dermis and subcutaneous tissue, typically caused by bacterial infection. It is a walled-off pocket of infection that requires drainage for resolution.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Bacteria (most commonly Staphylococcus aureus) invade your skin through a hair follicle or break in your skin. your immune system responds with neutrophils that accumulate at the site. As bacteria and dead neutrophils accumulate, pus forms. your body walls off the infection with a fibrous capsule, preventing the spread of bacteria. The increasing pressure causes pain and fluctuation.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Bacterial infection: Staphylococcus aureus (including MRSA)
\item Streptococcus pyogenes
\item Anaerobic bacteria (in areas with poor circulation)
\item Blocked sweat gland or hair follicle
\item Foreign body
\item Intravenous drug use
\item Diabetes mellitus
\item Immunosuppression
\item Poor hygiene
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
\begin{itemize}
\item Tender, red, swollen nodule under the skin
\item Warmth and erythema of the overlying skin
\item Fluctuance on palpation (feeling of fluid)
\item Pain and tenderness
\item Possible overlying pustule or draining sinus
\item Fever and malaise in systemic infection
\item Enlarging lymph nodes near the abscess
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
\item Cellulitis (surrounding infection)
\item Boil (furuncle, infected hair follicle)
\item Carbuncle (coalescence of multiple furuncles)
\item Hidradenitis suppurativa (chronic abscesses in apocrine glands)
\item Sepsis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item Apply warm compresses to promote drainage
\item Do NOT squeeze or puncture the abscess
\item Keep the area clean
\item Take OTC pain relievers
\item Seek medical care if fluctuant or systemic symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clinical examination
\item Ultrasound to confirm fluid collection
\item Wound culture and sensitivity
\item Blood cultures if systemic infection suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
\item Incision and drainage (I and D) is the primary treatment
\item Packing of the wound cavity
\item Antibiotics: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole or clindamycin (for MRSA coverage)
\item Warm compresses and wound care
\item Pain management
\end{itemize}

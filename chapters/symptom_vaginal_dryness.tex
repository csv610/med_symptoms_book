\chapter{Vaginal Dryness}

\section*{Definition}
Insufficient vaginal lubrication causing discomfort, irritation, and pain during sexual activity or daily activities, most commonly due to decreased estrogen levels.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Estrogen maintains vaginal mucosal thickness, elasticity, and lubrication via cervical and Bartholin gland secretions. Estrogen decline (menopause, breastfeeding, medications) leads to mucosal atrophy, decreased blood flow, reduced secretions, and increased vaginal pH predisposing to infection. Vaginal dryness is symptomatic of the genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM).

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Menopause (natural or surgical)
  \item Breastfeeding or postpartum period
  \item Medication effects (antihistamines, antidepressants, chemotherapy, tamoxifen)
  \item Sjogren syndrome (autoimmune)
  \item Radiation therapy to pelvis
  \item Hormonal contraception (some types)
  \item Perimenopause
  \item Oophorectomy (surgical removal of ovaries)
  \item Anxiety or inadequate arousal
  \item Chemotherapy or endocrine therapy for breast cancer
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Vaginal dryness with painful intercourse, itching, burning, or recurrent urinary tract infections
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Painful sex (pain during intercourse)
  \item Vaginal itching
  \item Urinary frequency
  \item Recurrent UTIs
  \item Vaginal irritation
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Use water-based or silicone-based vaginal lubricants generously before and during sexual activity to reduce friction and discomfort.
  \item Apply a vaginal moisturiser (such as Replens) every two to three days regularly, not just before sex, to maintain vaginal hydration and pH.
  \item Avoid harsh soaps, douches, and scented hygiene products in the genital area; wash only with warm water.
  \item Continue regular sexual activity or use a vaginal dilator to maintain vaginal elasticity and blood flow.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pelvic examination: assess vaginal mucosal thinning, pallor, loss of rugae; vaginal pH testing (pH greater than 5.0 suggests oestrogen deficiency); discharge sampling.
  \item Hormonal evaluation: serum oestradiol, FSH, and LH to confirm menopausal status if uncertain; TSH to rule out thyroid dysfunction.
  \item Rule out autoimmune causes: Schirmer test for Sjogren syndrome if dry eyes and dry mouth are also present; anti-SSA (Ro) and anti-SSB (La) antibodies.
  \item Wet mount microscopy to distinguish atrophic vaginitis (parabasal cells, increased pH) from infection (yeast, bacterial vaginosis, trichomonas).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item First-line: non-hormonal vaginal moisturisers applied regularly and lubricants for intercourse.
  \item Low-dose vaginal oestrogen therapy: oestradiol vaginal cream, ring, or tablet with minimal systemic absorption; for women with genitourinary syndrome of menopause.
  \item Systemic hormone therapy (oral or transdermal oestrogen with progestogen if the uterus is intact) for women with additional menopausal symptoms.
  \item Ospemifene 60 mg daily (oral selective oestrogen receptor modulator) as a non-hormonal alternative for moderate-to-severe dyspareunia due to vulvovaginal atrophy.
\end{itemize}

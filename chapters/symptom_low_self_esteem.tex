\chapter{Low Self-Esteem (Loss of Confidence)}

\section*{Definition}
A negative perception of self-worth and abilities, often accompanied by self-doubt, social withdrawal, and difficulty asserting oneself in personal or professional settings.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Self-esteem is influenced by prefrontal cortical regulation of amygdala and limbic system responses to social evaluation. Oestrogen modulates serotonin and dopamine neurotransmission; declining oestrogen reduces serotonergic tone, increasing sensitivity to negative social feedback and self-criticism. Physical symptoms of menopause (hot flushes, weight gain, skin/hair changes) may alter body image and self-perception. Sleep disruption and fatigue impair cognitive function, further undermining confidence in work and social roles.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Perimenopausal and menopausal changes (physical and emotional)
  \item Major depressive disorder
  \item Generalised anxiety disorder
  \item Social anxiety disorder
  \item Body image disturbance (weight gain, skin/hair changes)
  \item Chronic illness or pain
  \item Workplace stress or discrimination
  \item Relationship difficulties or divorce
  \item History of trauma or abuse
  \item Perfectionism and high self-standards
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Low self-esteem with depressed mood, social withdrawal, work impairment, or thoughts of self-harm
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Depression
  \item Anxiety
  \item Mood swings
  \item Fatigue
  \item Body image concerns
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Practise daily self-compassion by writing down three things you did well or appreciated about yourself, no matter how small.
  \item Identify and challenge negative self-talk using a thought diary — record the situation, automatic thought, and a more balanced alternative.
  \item Set small, achievable goals in areas you value (work, hobbies, relationships) and acknowledge each accomplishment to rebuild confidence gradually.
  \item Limit social media use, particularly comparison-driven content, and curate your feed to include positive, realistic accounts.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Structured clinical interview assessing self-perception, social functioning, work performance, and screening for depression, anxiety, and eating disorders.
  \item Standardised questionnaires (Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale, PHQ-9, GAD-7) to quantify severity and identify comorbid conditions.
  \item Thyroid function tests and hormonal profile (FSH, oestradiol) if perimenopausal symptoms are present and may be contributing.
  \item Referral to a psychologist or psychiatrist is indicated for moderate-to-severe low self-esteem, particularly when associated with depression, social withdrawal, or impaired daily function.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is the most evidence-based treatment — it targets the negative automatic thoughts and core beliefs that maintain low self-esteem.
  \item Menopausal hormone therapy may improve self-esteem indirectly by alleviating physical symptoms (hot flushes, sleep disturbance, weight distribution) that affect body image.
  \item Group therapy or peer support groups provide validation and reduce shame by normalising shared experiences of low confidence.
  \item Mindfulness-based interventions help reduce self-critical rumination and increase self-compassion through present-moment awareness.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

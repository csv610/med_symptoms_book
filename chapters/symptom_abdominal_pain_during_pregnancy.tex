\chapter{Abdominal Pain During Pregnancy}

\section*{Definition}
Abdominal pain during pregnancy is discomfort anywhere between the chest and pelvis in a pregnant person. Mild, brief pain can result from normal stretching, gas, constipation, or uterine contractions, but severe, persistent, localized, or bleeding-associated pain may indicate a pregnancy complication or a non-obstetric emergency.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Pregnancy enlarges and changes the uterus, abdominal organs, blood vessels, and pelvic ligaments. Hormonal effects slow bowel movement and relax smooth muscle. As pregnancy progresses, stretching of the round ligaments and pressure from the uterus can cause pain. Dangerous causes can involve placental separation, ectopic pregnancy, uterine rupture, infection, appendicitis, gallbladder disease, or intestinal obstruction.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Round-ligament pain, Braxton Hicks contractions, constipation, gas, reflux, or musculoskeletal strain
  \item Ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage, placental abruption, preterm labor, or uterine rupture
  \item Urinary tract infection, kidney stone, pyelonephritis, or ovarian torsion
  \item Appendicitis, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, gastroenteritis, or bowel obstruction
  \item Preeclampsia or HELLP syndrome when upper-right abdominal pain occurs with headache, visual symptoms, or high blood pressure
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
Seek emergency care for severe or worsening pain, fainting, shoulder-tip pain, vaginal bleeding, fluid leakage, fever, repeated vomiting, painful urination with fever, regular contractions before term, reduced fetal movement, chest pain, severe headache, visual changes, or upper-right pain with swelling or high blood pressure.

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Vaginal bleeding, pelvic pressure, contractions, fluid leakage, or reduced fetal movement
  \item Fever, vomiting, diarrhea, urinary burning, flank pain, or jaundice
  \item Headache, visual disturbance, sudden swelling, or shortness of breath
\end{itemize}

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
Mild pain that clearly improves with rest or position change may be monitored briefly. Hydrate, avoid strenuous activity, and use only pregnancy-safe medicines recommended by a clinician. Do not use laxatives or pain medicines to mask severe pain, and do not delay obstetric assessment.

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item History includes gestational age, pain location and onset, bleeding, contractions, fetal movement, urinary and gastrointestinal symptoms, trauma, and prior pregnancy complications.
  \item Examination assesses vital signs, abdominal tenderness, uterine activity, fetal heart rate, hydration, and signs of shock or infection.
  \item Testing may include urine studies, blood count, liver tests, lipase, pregnancy-related labs, ultrasound, fetal monitoring, and MRI or CT when clinically necessary.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
Treatment is cause-specific and may include hydration, observation, antibiotics, treatment of urinary disease, management of preeclampsia, or urgent obstetric, surgical, or neonatal care. Imaging and treatment decisions balance maternal and fetal safety without withholding necessary emergency care.

\chapter{Snoring}

\section*{Definition}
A coarse, harsh breathing sound produced during sleep by vibration of the soft palate, uvula, pharyngeal walls, and tongue base due to partial upper airway blockage, ranging from benign primary snoring to a marker of obstructive sleep apnea (OSA).

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
During sleep, reduced muscle tone in the pharyngeal dilator muscles allows the soft palate, uvula, and tongue to collapse partially into the airway. Airflow through the narrowed oropharynx creates negative pressure causing vibration of floppy soft tissue structures. Factors increasing collapse risk include obesity (fat deposition around airway), supine position, alcohol/sedatives (muscle relaxation), and anatomical narrowing (large tonsils, retrognathia, macroglossia).

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Obesity (most common modifiable risk factor)
  \item Supine sleeping position (gravity worsens airway collapse)
  \item Alcohol, sedatives, or muscle relaxants before sleep
  \item Nasal congestion or blockage (allergies, deviated septum, polyps)
  \item Enlarged tonsils or adenoids (common in children)
  \item Retrognathia or micrognathia (small or recessed jaw)
  \item Macroglossia (large tongue, seen in Down syndrome, acromegaly)
  \item Hypothyroidism (myxedematous changes, obesity, muscle dysfunction)
  \item Menopause (hormonal changes affecting airway muscle tone)
  \item Aging (decreased pharyngeal muscle tone with age)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Loud snoring with witnessed breathing pauses, choking/gasping during sleep, excessive daytime sleepiness, morning headaches, or hypertension (possible sleep apnea)
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Daytime sleepiness
  \item Fatigue
  \item Morning headache
  \item Sleep apnea
  \item Restless sleep
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Lose weight if BMI exceeds 25: even a 5--10\% reduction in body weight significantly reduces pharyngeal fat and airway collapsibility.
  \item Sleep on your side rather than your back: use a body pillow, a tennis ball sewn into a shirt pocket (positional therapy), or a wedge pillow to maintain lateral decubitus position.
  \item Avoid alcohol, sedatives, and heavy meals within three hours of bedtime, as these relax pharyngeal muscles and promote airway collapse.
  \item Address nasal congestion with saline rinses, intranasal corticosteroid sprays (fluticasone), or nasal dilator strips to reduce inspiratory resistance.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Overnight polysomnography (sleep study) is the gold standard to diagnose obstructive sleep apnoea; it measures the apnoea-hypopnoea index, oxygen desaturation nadir, sleep architecture, and limb movements.
  \item Home sleep apnoea test for patients with a high pre-test probability of moderate-to-severe OSA without significant comorbidities.
  \item Otolaryngology evaluation with nasopharyngoscopy to identify anatomical obstruction sites: nasal cavity, palate, tongue base, or hypopharynx.
  \item ENT referral if craniofacial abnormalities (retrognathia, tonsillar hypertrophy, macroglossia) are contributing; consider drug-induced sleep endoscopy for surgical planning.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) is first-line therapy for moderate-to-severe OSA (AHI greater than or equal to 15); titrate pressure during polysomnography.
  \item Oral appliance therapy (mandibular advancement device) for mild-to-moderate OSA or for CPAP-intolerant patients; custom-fitted by a dental sleep specialist.
  \item Positional therapy for supine-dependent OSA (AHI in supine is at least double that in non-supine).
  \item Surgical options: uvulopalatopharyngoplasty, tonsillectomy, nasal surgery, or hypoglossal nerve stimulation for selected patients.
\end{itemize}

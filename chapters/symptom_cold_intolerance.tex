\chapter{Cold Intolerance (Feeling Cold)}

\section*{Definition}
An exaggerated sensitivity to cold temperatures or a persistent sensation of feeling cold even in normal environmental conditions, often with visible narrowing of blood vessels of the extremities.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Cold intolerance results from altered thermoregulation, reduced metabolic heat production, or impaired vasomotor control. Oestrogen influences hypothalamic thermoregulatory set-point; declining oestrogen destabilises the thermoregulatory centre, causing both hot flushes and cold spells. Reduced basal metabolic rate (age-related and thyroid-related) decreases heat production. Vasomotor symptoms alternate between widening of blood vessels (hot flushes) and narrowing of blood vessels (cold flushes) as the hypothalamus attempts to regulate temperature.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Perimenopausal vasomotor instability (cold flushes)
  \item Hypothyroidism (reduced metabolic rate)
  \item Iron deficiency anaemia (impaired oxygen delivery)
  \item Peripheral arterial disease
  \item Raynaud phenomenon or disease
  \item Low body weight or inadequate caloric intake
  \item Anorexia nervosa (loss of insulating fat)
  \item Vitamin B12 deficiency (impaired myelination)
  \item Diabetes mellitus (peripheral neuropathy)
  \item Medications (beta-blockers, ergot derivatives)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}
See your doctor if:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
  \item Persistent cold intolerance with fatigue, weight gain, constipation, dry skin, or colour changes in fingers/toes (blue/white)
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fatigue
  \item Cold hands and feet
  \item Weight gain
  \item Dry skin
  \item Hot flushes
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} If this symptom persists, your doctor will check for serious underlying causes.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Dress in warm layers, use thermal underwear, and keep your home at a comfortable temperature (20–22°C) to manage cold sensitivity.
  \item Warm up gradually with warm baths, heated blankets, or hot water bottles — avoid sudden temperature extremes.
  \item Stay physically active with regular exercise to boost circulation and metabolic heat production.
  \item Ensure adequate caloric and nutritional intake, including iron-rich foods (red meat, spinach, fortified cereals) and vitamin B12 sources.
  \item Avoid smoking and limit alcohol, both of which can impair circulation and worsen cold intolerance.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Your doctor will assess for signs of hypothyroidism (fatigue, weight gain, dry skin, constipation, bradycardia) and perform a thyroid gland examination.
  \item Laboratory evaluation includes thyroid function tests (TSH, free T4), complete blood count, iron studies, vitamin B12 and folate levels, and basic metabolic panel.
  \item A thyroid ultrasound is arranged if the thyroid gland is abnormal on palpation (nodules, goitre) or if thyroid function tests are abnormal.
  \item Referral to an endocrinologist is indicated for persistent cold intolerance with abnormal thyroid function, suspected pituitary dysfunction, or when initial workup is unrevealing.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Levothyroxine replacement therapy is prescribed for hypothyroidism, with dose titration based on TSH levels every 6–8 weeks until stable.
  \item Iron supplementation (oral ferrous sulfate or ferrous fumarate) is initiated for iron deficiency anaemia, with repeat haemoglobin and ferritin monitoring.
  \item Hormone replacement therapy (oestrogen-based) may reduce vasomotor symptoms including cold flushes in perimenopausal women if hormonal fluctuation is the underlying cause.
  \item Lifestyle measures including regular aerobic exercise, adequate nutrition, and smoking cessation are maintained as adjunctive therapy regardless of the underlying cause.
  \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
  \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Akathisia}

\section*{Definition}
Akathisia is a movement disorder characterized by subjective inner restlessness and an irresistible urge to move, accompanied by objective repetitive movements such as rocking, pacing, crossing/uncrossing legs, or shifting weight while standing. It is most commonly associated with antipsychotic medication use and is classified as acute (within weeks) or tardive (after months to years).

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
The mechanism is not fully understood but is believed to involve dopamine D2 receptor blockade in the mesocortical pathway, particularly in the prefrontal cortex and anterior cingulate gyrus. Reduced dopaminergic transmission in the ventral tegmental area disrupts cortical-striatal-thalamic circuits that regulate motor planning and inhibition. Serotonergic (5-HT2A) and noradrenergic systems also modulate akathisia, which is why low-potency antipsychotics and certain antidepressants can trigger it. Iron deficiency in the basal ganglia has been implicated in some cases.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Antipsychotics:} First-generation (haloperidol, chlorpromazine) > second-generation (risperidone, olanzapine, aripiprazole)
 \item \textbf{Antidepressants:} SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, MAO inhibitors
 \item \textbf{Antiemetics:} Metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, droperidol
 \item \textbf{Calcium channel blockers:} Flunarizine, cinnarizine
 \item \textbf{Dopamine-depleting agents:} Tetrabenazine, reserpine
 \item \textbf{Other:} Lithium, buspirone, benzodiazepine withdrawal
 \item \textbf{Medical conditions:} Parkinson disease, restless legs syndrome, iron deficiency anemia
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
 \item Subjective inner restlessness developing after starting or increasing a dopamine-blocking medication
 \item Objective fidgeting, pacing, or inability to sit still
 \item Distress severe enough to affect sleep, work, or social function
 \item Akathisia with suicidal ideation (akathisia-associated suicidality is a known risk)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Subjective restlessness (inner tension, anxiety, dysphoria)
 \item Objective motor agitation (rocking, shifting, crossing/uncrossing legs, pacing)
 \item Inability to maintain a seated or standing posture
 \item Sleep disturbance and irritability
 \item Dyskinesia, dystonia, or parkinsonism if also drug-induced
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Important:} Akathisia is often misdiagnosed as agitation, anxiety, or worsening psychosis; the Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale (BARS) is the validated tool for objective assessment.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Do not stop antipsychotic medication abruptly; consult the prescribing provider first
 \item Avoidance of caffeine, stimulants, and other exacerbating substances
 \item Regular physical activity to channel the restlessness (walking, stretching)
 \item Relaxation techniques: deep breathing, guided imagery, mindfulness
 \item Cool environment and loose clothing to reduce physical discomfort
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Clinical diagnosis using the Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale (BARS)
 \item Medication history with timeline of symptom onset relative to drug initiation, dose change, or withdrawal
 \item Physical examination: observe patient during sitting, standing, and lying still
 \item Differentiate from restless legs syndrome (worse at night, relieved by movement), anxiety (lack of objective movement), tardive dyskinesia (involuntary, not driven by urge), and psychomotor agitation
 \item Serum ferritin and iron studies if restless legs overlap is suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Reduce dose of offending antipsychotic or switch to a lower-potency agent (quetiapine, clozapine)
 \item Beta-blockers (propranolol 30--80 mg/day) as first-line pharmacologic treatment
 \item Benzodiazepines (clonazepam, lorazepam) for acute symptom relief
 \item Anticholinergics (benztropine) if concurrent parkinsonism
 \item Mirtazapine 15 mg at bedtime for refractory akathisia
 \item Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) 600 mg/day has shown benefit in some studies
 \item Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) for severe, treatment-resistant akathisia with underlying psychiatric illness
\end{itemize}

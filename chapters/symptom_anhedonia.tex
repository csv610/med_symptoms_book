\chapter{Anhedonia (Loss of Interest)}

\section*{Definition}
The reduced ability to experience pleasure or interest in activities that were previously enjoyable, a core symptom of depression that may also occur independently during hormonal transitions.

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Anhedonia involves dysfunction of your brain reward circuitry, particularly the mesolimbic dopamine pathway projecting from the ventral tegmental area to the nucleus accumbens and prefrontal cortex. Oestrogen modulates dopamine receptor density and sensitivity; declining oestrogen during perimenopause reduces dopaminergic tone, blunting reward perception. Serotonergic and noradrenergic systems are also affected, contributing to diminished anticipation and experience of pleasure.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Major depressive disorder (most common association)
 \item Perimenopausal and menopausal hormonal changes
 \item Chronic stress (elevated cortisol suppressing dopamine)
 \item Schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder
 \item Substance use disorders (dopamine receptor downregulation)
 \item Parkinson disease (dopamine depletion)
 \item Post-traumatic stress disorder
 \item Medication side effects (SSRIs, antipsychotics)
 \item Hypothyroidism
 \item Vitamin D deficiency
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
 \item Persistent loss of interest lasting more than 2 weeks, especially with depressed mood, sleep or appetite changes, or suicidal thoughts
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Depression
 \item Fatigue
 \item Low mood
 \item Mood swings
 \item Difficulty concentrating
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} Your doctor will check for serious underlying conditions when evaluating persistent anhedonia.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Establish a daily routine with small, achievable goals such as a short walk, a hobby for 10 minutes, or completing one household task.
 \item Prioritise sleep hygiene: maintain consistent bedtimes, limit screen exposure before sleep, and avoid alcohol or caffeine in the evening.
 \item Engage in social connection even when it feels difficult — brief conversations with trusted friends or family can reduce isolation.
 \item Avoid making major life decisions while experiencing anhedonia, as impaired reward processing may skew judgement.
 \item Track mood and interest levels daily to identify patterns and share these with your healthcare provider.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Your doctor will take a detailed psychiatric history including duration of anhedonia, associated mood symptoms, sleep and appetite changes, and suicidal ideation.
 \item Screening questionnaires such as the PHQ-9 or Snaith-Hamilton Pleasure Scale (SHAPS) are used to quantify anhedonia severity.
 \item Laboratory evaluation includes thyroid function tests, vitamin B12 and D levels, iron studies, and a complete blood count to identify reversible medical causes.
 \item Referral to a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist is arranged for comprehensive assessment and psychotherapy, particularly if depression or another mood disorder is suspected.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Antidepressant therapy, particularly with SSRIs or SNRIs, is first-line pharmacotherapy for anhedonia associated with depression.
 \item Augmentation strategies including bupropion, aripiprazole, or cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) may be used when anhedonia is resistant to initial treatment.
 \item Behavioural activation therapy, a structured CBT approach that schedules rewarding activities, directly targets anhedonia by rebuilding reward circuitry.
 \item Hormonal therapy (oestrogen replacement) may be considered for perimenopausal women when anhedonia correlates with hormonal decline.
 \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
 \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}

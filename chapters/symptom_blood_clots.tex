\chapter{Blood Clots (Venous Thromboembolism)}

\section*{Definition}
Formation of a blood clot (thrombus) within a deep vein, most commonly in the lower extremities (deep vein thrombosis), which may dislodge and travel to the lungs (pulmonary embolism).

\section*{What Happens in Your Body}
Virchow triad describes predisposing factors: venous stasis (immobility, surgery, long travel), endothelial injury (trauma, surgery, catheterization), and hypercoagulability (inherited thrombophilia, malignancy, pregnancy, oral contraceptives). DVT typically starts in the calf veins and may propagate proximally. Embolization to pulmonary arteries causes ventilation-perfusion mismatch, increased pulmonary vascular resistance, and potentially hemodynamic collapse.

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Prolonged immobility (surgery, hospitalization, long-haul travel)
 \item Malignancy (cancer-associated thrombosis)
 \item Oral contraceptives or hormone replacement therapy
 \item Pregnancy and postpartum period
 \item Inherited thrombophilia (Factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation)
 \item Antiphospholipid syndrome
 \item Trauma or major surgery (especially orthopedic)
 \item Central venous catheters
 \item Obesity
 \item Advanced age
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to See a Doctor}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Symptoms are severe or getting worse over time
 \item Unilateral leg swelling, warmth, and redness (possible DVT), or sudden chest pain, shortness of breath, and coughing up blood (possible PE) — seek emergency care
  \item You have other concerning symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Related Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Leg swelling
 \item Chest pain
 \item Shortness of breath
 \item Calf pain
 \item coughing up blood
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Important:} Your doctor will check for serious underlying conditions when evaluating persistent blood clots.

\section*{Self-Care / Home Management}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Seek emergency medical care if you have sudden leg swelling, warmth, and redness (possible DVT) or sudden chest pain, shortness of breath, or coughing up blood (possible PE).
 \item Do not massage or apply heat to a swollen, painful leg as this may dislodge a clot and cause pulmonary embolism.
 \item Stay well hydrated and mobilise as tolerated during long journeys (walk every 2 hours, ankle pumps, and calf stretches while seated).
 \item If already diagnosed with a DVT, wear compression stockings as prescribed and elevate the affected leg when resting.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How Your Doctor Will Evaluate You}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Your doctor will assess for unilateral leg swelling, calf tenderness, Homans sign, and measure leg circumference compared to the contralateral limb.
 \item D-dimer blood test is performed as an initial screening test — a negative D-dimer essentially rules out DVT/PE in low-risk patients.
 \item Compression duplex ultrasound of the proximal leg veins is the first-line imaging for suspected DVT; CT pulmonary angiography is the gold standard for PE.
 \item Referral to a haematologist is arranged for hypercoagulability workup (Factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation, antiphospholipid antibodies) in patients under 50 with unprovoked clots.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment Options}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Anticoagulation with direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban) or warfarin is initiated immediately for confirmed DVT or PE.
 \item Low-molecular-weight heparin (enoxaparin) is used for initial anticoagulation in pregnancy, cancer-associated thrombosis, or while transitioning to warfarin.
 \item Thrombolytic therapy or catheter-directed thrombolysis is reserved for massive PE with haemodynamic instability or extensive iliofemoral DVT.
 \item Inferior vena cava filter is placed in patients with contraindications to anticoagulation or recurrent PE despite adequate anticoagulation.
 \item Medications, lifestyle modifications, and physical therapies may be prescribed as appropriate.
 \item Follow-up ensures treatment effectiveness and allows timely adjustments to the care plan.
\end{itemize}
